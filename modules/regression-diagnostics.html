01The problem

Why can a high R-squared coexist with a bad model?

R² describes how much the fitted model reduced squared spread on a dataset. It does not certify that the model or its assumptions are suitable. Models with similar R² can miss very different patterns.

Why this works · a little deeper

A model can return a number for every row even when its shape or uncertainty assumptions are poor. Diagnostics are checks, not automatic pass/fail proofs. Inspect the raw data, fitted predictions, residuals, sampling design and intended use together.

04Reasoning with the method

Which diagnostic answers which question?

Choose the plot for the question. Residual plots show missed shape and spread. Q–Q plots compare error distributions. Leverage and VIF check different input issues. No single picture checks everything.

Why this works · a little deeper

A residual-versus-fitted plot checks pattern and spread. A Q–Q plot compares ordered residuals with a reference such as a normal distribution. Normal predictors are not required just because a test uses normal errors. Cook's distance screens influence, not automatic deletion. VIF measures how much a predictor overlaps the others; it can help explain wide coefficient intervals without proving bias.

Quick check · Must predictors themselves be normal for ordinary least squares?

No. Normal-error assumptions concern some forms of inference, not predictor normality.

06Assumptions and failure modes

What cannot a residual plot establish?

A plain-looking residual plot cannot prove that observations are independent. It cannot prove a causal effect either. Those claims depend on how data was collected and on further assumptions.

Why this works · a little deeper

Assumptions

Check the mean form and error structure needed by your chosen analysis. Independent errors, equal spread and normal errors are assumptions of particular classical methods, not properties proved by a plot.

When the method fails

Repeated people, time dependence or groups can give related errors even in an ordinary-looking scatterplot. Normal-error assumptions matter for some classical small-sample inference, not for every use of a regression prediction. Constant variance is also an assumption of particular uncertainty methods, not proof supplied by a tidy plot.

Quick check · Can a clean residual plot repair a bad sampling design?

No.

Why this works · a little deeper

What to report: Report coefficients with units and intervals, residual standard error, diagnostic findings and justified sensitivity results. Quick check · Which interval is usually wider at the same input?

The prediction interval, because it includes individual outcome variation.

Next: Knowledge checks ↓
09Knowledge checks

Can I match symptom and response?

Match the action to the evidence. Check unusual observations and measurement errors. Keep genuine observations unless there is a sound reason to exclude them. A nicer score is not enough reason.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Curved residualsInvestigate mean form
Repeated unitsModel dependence
Data-entry errorCorrect against source evidence
A widening residual funnel indicates…

Answer and explanation

Unequal error spread (heteroscedasticity). Residual variance changes with the fitted value.

Leverage differs from influence because…

Answer and explanation

Leverage describes unusual predictor position; influence describes effect on the fit. Influence combines position and discrepancy to measure how much the fit changes.

For one future observation use…

Answer and explanation

A prediction interval. Prediction intervals include mean-estimation uncertainty and individual noise.

A curved residual pattern suggests…

Answer and explanation

A shape in the average relationship the model missed. The mean relationship has not been represented adequately.

A high R² proves…

Answer and explanation

None of a correct model, a causal effect, or reliable use in a different population. R² alone cannot validate the model.

Why this works · a little deeper

Do not remove an observation just because it lowers R² or prevents a small p-value. Verify measurements and inclusion rules. Explain any excluded cases, transformations, dependence handling and remaining limits. The scientific question decides whether a change is useful.

Quick check · A valid rare observation shifts the fit. What should you report?

The observation's context and a sensitivity analysis, rather than silently deleting it.
