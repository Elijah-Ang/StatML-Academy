What does the model learn from?

An email becomes a row of measured clues plus its known label. Our teaching training sample has 40 spam emails and 60 ordinary emails. The class proportions supply the starting beliefs.

Why this works · a little deeper

Supervised learning uses labels supplied by a defined process; labels may still contain mistakes. The training examples should resemble the messages the filter will encounter. A prior P(class) is a probability before the current message’s measured clues are considered. Here we estimate it from the training class frequency. If data were sampled with an artificial class ratio, that ratio may not match deployment and needs justified correction.

Quick check · If every clue is ignored, what is P(spam) in this example?

40 / 100 = 0.40. The absence of measured evidence leaves the prior unchanged.

02Separate features from the answer

What can the filter know at prediction time?

The words are inputs. The spam label is the answer used for training, not a clue supplied when classifying a new email. For a binary feature, present means 1 and absent means 0.

Try it

Change “free” from present to absent, then to ignore. These are three different questions.

Why this works · a little deeper

Absent is evidence: it contributes P(word absent | class). Ignored means the feature is not observed for this calculation and contributes no factor. A full Bernoulli model normally includes both the present and absent terms for all vocabulary features. This visual explicitly allows partial evidence so you can build the calculation one clue at a time. It never silently treats an absent word as an ignored one. Tokenization and vocabulary selection belong inside training data, just like fitting class probabilities.

Quick check · Can “the user later marked this as spam” be a prediction-time feature?

Not if that action happens after prediction. It would leak the outcome into the inputs.

Why this works · a little deeper

The entire text-processing pipeline must be refit in each training fold. Building a vocabulary or choosing words from all messages before validation lets held-out information affect development. Split by sender, thread or time when random rows would mix related messages or reveal the future. An illustrative 80/20 picture is not a universally optimal ratio; sufficient independent examples and a deployment-matched design matter more. The live posterior here is an arithmetic demonstration, not a measured test accuracy.

Quick check · Should the final test help decide the smoothing strength?

No. That is a development choice. Choose it with validation or cross-validation, then evaluate once on the protected test.

Next: Choose a likelihood for the feature ↓
04Choose a likelihood for the feature

Why are there several kinds of Naive Bayes?

The family shares one probability recipe. The feature representation decides what kind of likelihood belongs in that recipe: binary presence, counts, categories or continuous measurements.

VariantFeature representation
BernoulliPresent / absent word
MultinomialWord occurrence counts
CategoricalOne of several named levels
GaussianA continuous measurement
↳
Try it

Choose a variant to inspect its representation. The email posterior lab remains explicitly Bernoulli.

Why this works · a little deeper

Multinomial Naive Bayes models feature counts with class-specific event probabilities; it does not use the same binary absence factors as Bernoulli. Categorical Naive Bayes models each feature’s discrete levels. Gaussian Naive Bayes fits a mean and variance for each continuous feature within each class. Match the likelihood to the measurement process and assess held-out behavior. Merely choosing a different name does not convert binary word counts into continuous observations.

Quick check · Is a word appearing three times identical to binary “present”?

Not as a representation. A count feature distinguishes one occurrence from three; binary presence does not.

Next: Count within each class ↓
05Count within each class

What does “given spam” mean?

Restrict attention to the spam emails, then count the clue. “Free” appears in 30 of 40 spam messages and 6 of 60 ordinary messages. These likelihoods are different from P(spam | free).

ClueSpam: count / 40Not spam: count / 60
free30 / 406 / 60
winner24 / 403 / 60
meeting2 / 4030 / 60
P(free | spam) ≠ P(spam | free)
↳
Try it

Use only the “free” clue. Compare its two likelihoods with the final posterior.

Why this works · a little deeper

Conditioning changes the denominator. P(free | spam) divides by the number of spam messages, whereas P(spam | free) divides by the number of messages containing “free.” Bayes’ rule connects these quantities through the prior and normalizing evidence. With additive smoothing α, a binary likelihood is (count + α) / (class count + 2α). There are two outcomes, present and absent, hence 2α. The live receipt uses the selected α; the table above is the raw training count before smoothing.

Quick check · Why is 30/40 not already P(spam | free)?

Its denominator includes all spam messages. We have conditioned on spam, not on the observed word.

Next: Name the naive assumption ↓
06Name the naive assumption

Why can we multiply the clues?

The model treats the features as independent after the class is known. This makes fitting manageable. In real language, clues can remain related even within a class.

P(clues | class) ≈ ∏ P(clue | class)

“Free” copied into a second feature is the same evidence twice. Toggle the duplicate to see confidence change without receiving a new fact.

↳
Try it

Duplicate the “free” feature. Watch the model become more confident for the same underlying message.

Why this works · a little deeper

Conditional independence is not the claim that words are unrelated across the entire inbox. It is a factorization within each class. Correlated predictors can cause redundant evidence to be counted repeatedly; resulting probabilities may be overconfident even when classification rankings are useful. The duplicate feature is an explicit assumption stress test, not a recommended transformation. Validation and probability calibration can reveal practical consequences, but neither proves the assumption.

Quick check · Does a higher posterior after copying a feature mean we learned more?

No. We repeated information. The stronger posterior is an artifact of treating redundant features as independent.

Next: Turn scores into a posterior ↓
07Turn scores into a posterior

How does evidence become a probability?

Multiply each prior by that class’s clue likelihoods. Those are unnormalized scores. Divide the spam score by the sum of both scores to make probabilities that add to one.

StepLive result
Spam score0.175737
Not-spam score0.004370
P(spam | selected clues)97.6%

Smoothing α = 1. Ignored features contribute no evidence.

↳
Try it

Add “meeting” as present. Follow its likelihood factor through both scores before looking at the posterior.

Why this works · a little deeper

For classes c, Bayes gives P(c | x) = P(c)P(x | c) / ΣₖP(k)P(x | k). The denominator is shared across classes, so classification by the largest unnormalized score gives the same winner. Normalization is still necessary to display probabilities. These posteriors are conditional on the model and its estimated likelihoods; they are not guaranteed calibrated real-world risks. If every class assigns zero probability to the evidence, normalization is undefined. The visual reports this case rather than inventing a probability.

Quick check · Do class scores have to add to one before normalization?

No. They become posterior probabilities only after division by their sum.

08A continuous clue needs a density

What changes when the feature is a height?

Try it

Move the plant height marker through the overlap. Read the two density heights and normalized class probability.

Why this works · a little deeper

A continuous distribution assigns probability to an interval, not a single exact value. The curve’s height is a probability density, with inverse measurement units; it is not P(height equals exactly 170). Gaussian Naive Bayes multiplies class-conditional densities across features and normalizes class scores. Means, variances and class priors must be estimated from training data. Variance smoothing prevents numerical problems when a feature is nearly constant. This separate plant example assumes equal class priors and is not derived from the email counts.

Quick check · Is the density at 170 cm the probability of a plant being exactly 170 cm tall?

No. Probability is area over an interval. Density is a height used to compare relative support at that measurement.

Next: Avoid zeros and tiny-number trouble ↓
09Avoid zeros and tiny-number trouble

Why add smoothing and use logarithms?

An unseen word can give a class a zero likelihood, wiping out its entire product. Smoothing assigns some probability to unseen outcomes. Logs turn a long product into a sum without changing which class wins.

log score = log prior + Σ log likelihood

“Unicorn” appeared 0 times in spam and once in ordinary mail. Try it with α = 0, then α = 1.

↳
Try it

Set unicorn to present and smoothing to zero. Increase α and inspect the repaired likelihood.

Why this works · a little deeper

For Bernoulli features, α is added to both the present and absent counts, so the denominator gains 2α. In a multinomial word-count model, the denominator instead gains α times vocabulary size. Smoothing expresses a regularizing assumption and can alter probabilities; it is not merely a floating-point fix. Logs address underflow from multiplying many small numbers. Stable normalization subtracts the largest log score before exponentiation (the log-sum-exp idea). A zero likelihood has log probability −∞. Both classes being impossible is reported as undefined.

Quick check · Does switching from products to log sums itself change the predicted class?

No, apart from numerical precision. Log is monotonic. Smoothing can change the model, while logs change how its arithmetic is computed.

10Validate the whole recipe

What happens in one cross-validation round?

Train on four folds and validate on the fifth. Rotate the held-out fold so each development example is assessed by a model that did not train on it. Keep the final test outside this loop.

↳
Try it

Select each validation fold. Trace the held-out role as it moves across rounds.

Why this works · a little deeper

Cross-validation estimates the performance of a fitting procedure, not the quality of one fixed set of coefficients. Refit the vocabulary, any feature selection, and likelihood parameters inside each training fold. Choose an appropriate splitter: stratification can stabilize class counts, while grouped or time-ordered splits address dependence or chronology. Repeatedly searching many settings on the same validation estimates can overfit selection; nested CV or a protected final test addresses that final evaluation question. Report the resampling design and uncertainty, not only the best mean.

Quick check · May vocabulary selection run once on every fold before cross-validation?

No. If it learns from the data, fit it inside each training fold to keep validation independent.

11Choose a decision, not just a class

When should a message be flagged?

A posterior becomes an action only after choosing a threshold. Lower thresholds catch more spam but may flag more legitimate mail. Choose the trade-off on validation data using the consequences of each error.

ErrorPractical cost
False positiveAn ordinary email is hidden
False negativeSpam reaches the inbox

Current rule: flag if P(spam) ≥ 0.50.
Selected message: Flag as spam.

↳
Try it

Move the threshold past the current posterior. The evidence stays the same; the action changes.

Why this works · a little deeper

Precision asks how many flagged messages really are spam; recall asks how many spam messages were caught. Those population metrics require many labeled held-out predictions, not one posterior. If probabilities are calibrated and false-positive and false-negative costs are known, a simple two-action decision threshold can be derived from those costs. Real systems also have review capacity, delayed labels and distribution shift. Evaluate calibration separately from ranking, and lock the decision rule before final testing.

Quick check · Does changing the threshold retrain the word likelihoods?

No. It changes the action rule applied to the same estimated probabilities.

Next: Put the whole story together ↓
12Put the whole story together

What should you remember when using it?

Choose available features, protect evaluation data, match the likelihood, fit counts, inspect the assumption, then choose and validate a decision rule. Keep the arithmetic simple and the claims proportionate.

Use it when: a fast, interpretable probability baseline suits the feature representation.

Be careful when: evidence is strongly redundant, labels drift, rare categories dominate or probability calibration matters.

What to report: variant, feature construction, smoothing, split design, decision threshold, held-out metrics, calibration and limitations.

Why this works · a little deeper

Naive Bayes is often efficient to fit and predict, especially for sparse text representations. Its usefulness should be demonstrated relative to relevant baselines. Simplicity helps expose the model’s assumptions but does not make them true. A comprehensive report distinguishes training counts, validation choices and final test evidence. Check for rare or unseen feature values, class-prior shift, redundant evidence and preprocessing leakage.
