01The Raw Data

What do the labeled clouds represent?

Each dot is an example with a known class. LDA learns where each class is centred and how points spread around those centres. It uses those summaries to label a new point.

Next: Group by Class ↓
04Group by Class

Why group observations by class?

LDA models each class as a bell-shaped cloud and uses one shared spread shape. The centres can differ. That shared-shape assumption makes the model simpler, but may miss differences between classes.

Why this works · a little deeper

A multivariate Gaussian is a bell-shaped probability model in several inputs. Its contour is an equal-density outline, not a hard wall containing all points. LDA assumes the class covariance matrices are equal. Real data may differ from this model, so inspect predictions and calibration rather than treating the assumptions as verified by a tidy plot.

Quick check · Is LDA classification an unsupervised grouping method?

No.

05Compute Class Means

What is a class mean?

The class mean is the class's average position. For two inputs, average each input separately. The resulting pair gives the centre of that class's cloud.

Next: Within-Class Scatter ↓
06Within-Class Scatter

What does within-class scatter describe?

Covariance describes spread and tilt. Its diagonal entries measure each input's squared spread. Its other entries describe how the inputs vary together. The shape matters when judging a point's distance from a class centre.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Off-diagonalCovariance between features

Try it

Why this works · a little deeper

Covariance is a table of spread measurements. Positive covariance means the two inputs tend to rise together within a class; negative means one tends to fall as the other rises. Near-zero covariance means little linear co-movement, not no relationship of any kind. The diagonal gives variances, or average squared spread, rather than standard deviations.

Quick check · Does zero covariance always mean independence?

No.

Next: The Shared Covariance Assumption ↓
07The Shared Covariance Assumption

Why share one covariance estimate?

LDA combines the within-class spread estimates into one shared covariance. Both classes use that same shape at different centres. Sharing means fewer quantities to estimate, but imposes a restriction.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Why this works · a little deeper

Pooling combines within-class covariance estimates with weights based on their available degrees of freedom. It does not just calculate the total cloud's covariance around one grand centre. After moving both fitted centres to zero, LDA's two fitted outlines have the same shape. Observed class clouds may still have different raw shapes.

Quick check · Does pooling mean computing covariance around the overall mean?

No. That would also include between-class separation.

Next: Between-Class Scatter ↓
08Between-Class Scatter

What counts as between-class separation?

A large gap between class centres is useful only compared with the spread inside the classes. LDA looks for directions where the centres separate while points within each class stay relatively close together.

Why this works · a little deeper

Within-class scatter describes spread around each class centre. Between-class scatter describes separation between centres. LDA compares these after projection. The ratio J(w)=wᵀS_Bw/(wᵀS_Ww) rewards separation relative to within-class spread. This is not simply the direction with the largest overall data spread, as in PCA.

Quick check · Is the longest raw mean difference always the best standardized direction?

No.

Next: The Optimal Direction ↓
09The Optimal Direction

Why project along this direction?

Projection turns each two-input point into a position on one line. LDA chooses a direction that separates class centres relative to their internal spread. It uses both the centre gap and the covariance.

Why this works · a little deeper

For two classes, w is proportional to S_W⁻¹(μ₁−μ₂), when the needed inverse exists. The inverse adjusts for spread and tilt. A projected score is a weighted combination of the original inputs. It is not a third original measurement or automatically a probability. LDA projection can give at most K−1 separating dimensions for K classes.

Quick check · Is this necessarily the same direction as PC1?

No. PCA ignores labels and maximizes total variance.

Next: The Decision Boundary ↓
10The Decision Boundary

Why is the boundary linear?

LDA calculates one score for each class and picks the higher score. With shared covariance, the score comparison makes a straight boundary in a two-input plot. Class priors can move that boundary.

Why this works · a little deeper

The prior π_k is how common class k is before seeing this row's inputs. It may come from training proportions or an explicit choice appropriate to the task. LDA's score is xᵀΣ⁻¹μ_k − ½μ_kᵀΣ⁻¹μ_k + log(π_k). The common quadratic part cancels when comparing scores. The largest score wins. In a separate illustration, scores 2.31 and −1.44 choose the first class.

Quick check · Can unequal class priors move a linear boundary?

Yes.

Name which class is positive before reading TP, FP, TN and FN. A false positive predicts the positive class for an actual negative. A false negative misses an actual positive. Next: Assumptions & Limitations ↓
14Assumptions & Limitations

When is the shared-shape assumption costly?

A shared shape may be too restrictive when classes spread very differently. QDA allows separate shapes, but needs more estimates and often more data. Compare both on suitable validation data.

Why this works · a little deeper

LDA can struggle with misspecified class shapes, influential unusual points, highly redundant inputs or too little data for covariance estimation. An absence of outliers is not a formal requirement for computing the classifier. Shrinkage can stabilize covariance. QDA, logistic regression or other classifiers answer related prediction tasks with different assumptions; choose using comparable validation rather than a universal ranking.

Quick check · Is QDA automatically better whenever the shapes differ slightly?

No. Estimation cost can outweigh the flexibility.
