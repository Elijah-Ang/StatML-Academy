Next: Turn examples into tensors ↓
04Turn examples into tensors

What is a tensor's shape?

A tensor is an array of numbers with a stated shape. Its dimensions can mean batch, height, width and channels. Shape describes how the numbers are organized, not how similar the examples are.

Text tokenization splits text into pieces with IDs; an embedding maps an ID to a learned vector. Audio can use sampled amplitudes or frequency summaries over time. Preserve the declared order, dimensions and preparation at prediction time.

05Inside one neuron

What does one neuron do?

One neuron multiplies inputs by weights, adds a bias, and applies an activation. Larger networks repeat and combine that small operation. The calculation is the building block, not a magical decision.

Why this works · a little deeper

For one neuron, add weight×input products and the bias, then transform the sum. The bias is a starting offset. ReLU takes the larger of zero and the sum. A negative product is not automatically an absent input; it lowers the total. The diagram's products, sum and response must refer to the same selected values.

Quick check · Does a positive weight always make the final prediction larger?

06Depth builds features

Why might depth help?

Depth means more layers of transformations. Earlier responses become inputs for later ones. This can build useful representations, but does not guarantee neurons learn concepts with human names such as ears or eyes.

Why this works · a little deeper

A convolution filter multiplies a local input window by its weights and adds the products. Reusing it across positions creates a feature map. Deeper layers compose responses; their human descriptions are helpful interpretations, not guaranteed roles.

Next: How learning happens ↓
07How learning happens

How does the correction cycle work?

Training follows four steps: predict forward, calculate loss, calculate gradients, then update parameters. Keep the parameters unchanged until the gradients for that step are ready.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
ForwardPrediction
LossObjective value
BackwardParameter derivatives
UpdateNew parameters

↳
Try it

Follow one gold input through the hidden response maps and weighted contributions. Then advance through loss, gradient and update; rewind restores the earlier model.

Why this works · a little deeper

Forward uses the current parameters to make predictions. Loss compares them with targets. Backpropagation uses the chain rule to compute local slopes. The optimizer applies a step, with learning rate controlling its size. The gradient points uphill; its negative points locally downhill. A large step can make loss worse. One local diagram does not show every parameter of a large model.

Quick check · Is backpropagation itself the learning-rate rule?

No. It computes gradients used by an optimizer.

Different ways to reduce overfitting change different things. Dropout hides some activations during training. Augmentation varies examples. Weight decay penalizes weights. Early stopping selects an earlier checkpoint.

Match architecture to the information. A CNN computes local filter responses. An RNN updates a state through time. Attention uses normalized weights to combine information. Follow one actual calculation in each example.

Trace one complete local mechanism: CNN products and sum, RNN carried state, or attention’s weighted-average context vector.

Why this works · a little deeper

A CNN reuses learned filters across positions; it does not create a new filter for every image. An RNN passes a fixed-length state from one step to the next. Attention makes query–key scores, normalizes them into weights, then sums weighted values into context. Standard full attention can need memory growing quadratically with sequence length. These local diagrams expose mechanics, not a measured ranking of whole architectures.

10Start from a pretrained model

What is reused during transfer learning?

Transfer learning reuses parameters learned on an earlier task. We can freeze a backbone and train a new output head, then optionally adjust selected layers. The earlier task may still be a poor match for the new one.

Why this works · a little deeper

The backbone supplies reused features; the head maps them to the new target. Freeze means do not update those parameters. In this lab early features remain frozen, while a later backbone section may be trainable and the new head is trainable in both settings. Fine-tuning can adapt useful features, but source-data bias or a mismatched target can still hurt. Validate rather than assuming transfer is beneficial.

Quick check · Does freezing a backbone guarantee suitable features for the new task?
