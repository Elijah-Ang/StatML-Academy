The margin is the gap between two parallel support planes touching the closest training points on each side.

Margin boundaries: wᵀx + b = +1 and −1

Geometric margin width = 2 / ‖w‖

Maximising the margin is equivalent to keeping the weight vector small while still satisfying the classification constraints.

A wide margin can make the separator less sensitive. It cannot rescue biased data, leakage, poor labels, or a mismatched kernel. Geometry is useful, not divine intervention.

Why do support vectors matter?

Support vectors have nonzero dual weights and influence the fitted separator. They often lie on or within the margin; with overlap, some may be misclassified.

Support vectors lie on or inside the margin. They directly constrain the fitted boundary.

Only rows with αᵢ > 0 are support vectors.

Move a distant, safely classified point a little and the boundary may not change. Move a support vector and the solution can shift immediately.

Important distinction:

“support vector” means the row constrains this fitted margin. It does not mean the row is causally or commercially important in every possible sense.

A hard-margin SVM demands perfect separation with every row outside the margin. Real classes overlap, labels contain errors, and outliers appear because datasets enjoy sabotage.

A soft-margin SVM introduces slack variables ξᵢ so violations are allowed but penalised.

yᵢ(wᵀxᵢ + b) ≥ 1 − ξᵢ, with ξᵢ ≥ 0

SlackMeaning
ξ = 0Correct and on or beyond the margin
0 < ξ ≤ 1Correct side, but inside the margin
ξ > 1Misclassified

Small C

Violations are cheaper. The model accepts more errors to preserve a wider, smoother margin.

Large C

Violations are expensive. The model fits training rows more aggressively, often with a narrower margin.

C is an inverse regularisation parameter: larger C means less regularisation.

A huge C does not mean “more accurate”. It means “punish training violations more”, which can also mean “memorise the noise with confidence”.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

The decision function reports which side of the boundary a row occupies and how strongly it lies in that direction.

larger positive f(x) → stronger Class +1 decision

larger negative f(x) → stronger Class −1 decision

For a linear SVM, the score relates to signed distance after accounting for ‖w‖. For a kernel SVM, treat it as a boundary score rather than literal distance in the original plot.

f(x) = 2 does not mean 200%, nor twice the probability of f(x) = 1. Probability estimates require a separate calibration model fitted with held-out predictions.

Small gamma

Each point has broad influence. The decision surface changes slowly.

Large gamma

Influence becomes local. The boundary can wrap tightly around training points.

Large gamma plus large C can create an intricate boundary that remembers noise instead of learning structure.
