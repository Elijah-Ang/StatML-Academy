02Intuition and objective

What do a confidence interval and a p-value ask?

A confidence interval describes the long-run coverage of an interval procedure. A p-value describes a tail probability under a stated null model. Neither is a probability that the fixed null hypothesis is true.

How do we compute one interval and test?

For a mean with known population SD and independent normal observations, use SE = σ/√n, z = (estimate − null)/SE, and estimate ± 1.96 SE for a 95% interval. Estimated SD usually requires a t reference instead.

04Reasoning with the method

How do alpha, beta, power, and effect size connect?

Alpha controls false rejection under the null. Power is the probability of detecting a particular alternative under a planned design. It changes with effect size, sample size, variability, and the decision rule.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

A p-value is a tail probability calculated under H₀ and every model assumption; the null is conditioned on, not assigned a probability. A compatible confidence interval often inverts a family of tests. Sample size affects precision, so tiny effects can be statistically clear and important effects can remain uncertain. Power is planned against a specific meaningful alternative and is not the probability that an observed finding is true. Type I error means rejecting a true null hypothesis; α controls its probability under the test assumptions. Type II error means failing to reject a false null; its probability β depends on the particular alternative. Power = 1 − β is the chance of detecting that alternative. All else equal, lowering α generally lowers power, while larger sample sizes or larger effect sizes generally raise it. Family-wise and false-discovery-rate procedures answer different multiplicity goals. Neither repairs optional stopping, outcome switching or selective reporting after results are known.

Quick check · Is power the probability that an observed result is true?

No. Power is a prospective property for a specified alternative.

Why does larger n change precision?

With the same variability, increasing n shrinks SE and narrows the interval. It can make a small effect statistically detectable without making it practically useful.

06Assumptions and failure modes

When can the nominal result mislead?

Dependence, searching many outcomes, optional stopping, and a poor model can invalidate the advertised error rate. Changing the method after seeing the result also changes what the nominal p-value means.

09Knowledge checks

Can I interpret uncertainty correctly?

Interpret an interval and p-value together without replacing magnitude with a binary label. A result may be inconclusive, precise but small, or large but uncertain.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Estimate0.2 units
95% interval[0.1, 0.3]
Meaningful effect2 units
A 95% confidence procedure means…

Answer and explanation

Across repetitions, 95% of intervals capture the parameter under assumptions. Frequentist coverage is a long-run property of the procedure.

A p-value is not…

Answer and explanation

The probability the null is true. The null is conditioned on when calculating the p-value.

Testing 20 outcomes requires attention to…

Answer and explanation

Multiplicity. Multiple opportunities for small p-values inflate false positives.

Failing to reject means…

Answer and explanation

Evidence was insufficient at the chosen rule. Absence of sufficient evidence is not evidence of exact absence.

What completes an inferential result?

Answer and explanation

Effect size, interval and practical interpretation. Magnitude and uncertainty are required for useful interpretation.
