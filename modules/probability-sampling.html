01The problem

Why does a sample answer vary?

The population is the whole group we want to describe. Its mean is fixed once that group is defined. A sample is the smaller group we measure. Its mean can change when we choose different people.

Why this works · a little deeper

A parameter is a fixed number describing the chosen population, such as its true mean. A statistic is a number calculated from a sample. Before selecting the sample, that statistic can take different values. Repeating the same sampling method helps show how much it varies. It does not change the population parameter.

Quick check · Which changes when you draw another sample: parameter or statistic?

The statistic changes; the defined population parameter stays fixed.

02Intuition and objective

How are probability, density, and frequency different?

Probability describes chance. Frequency describes what happened in our sample. For separate possible values, add their probabilities. For a continuous curve, use the area over a range; the curve's height at one point is not a probability.

Why this works · a little deeper

P(A | B) means the chance of A among cases where B happened: P(A ∩ B)/P(B), when P(B)>0. The ∩ symbol means both events. Independence means knowing B does not change A's chance, so P(A ∩ B)=P(A)P(B). Expectation E[X] is an average weighted by probabilities. Variance E[(X−E[X])²] is the average squared gap from that average, when these quantities exist. Density uses area, and its height may exceed 1.

Quick check · Can a density curve be taller than one?

Yes. Its total area must be one; its height need not be.

03Worked example

Worked example

How is a standard error calculated?

Standard deviation, SD, describes how much individual values vary. Standard error, SE, describes how much an estimate varies across samples. For an independent sample mean, estimate SE by dividing sample SD by the square root of sample size.

Sample size36 people
Estimated SE12 / 6 = 2 points

Why this works · a little deeper

For SD 12 and n = 36, √36 = 6 because 6×6 = 36. Estimated SE = 12/6 = 2. This uses the independent-sampling formula. Four times the sample size doubles the square root, so it halves SE. Using the sample SD makes this an estimate, not an exactly known population SE.

Quick check · What happens to SE when n quadruples, with SD fixed?

It halves.

04Reasoning with the method

What is the sampling distribution?

Draw one sample and calculate its average. Place one dot for that average on another plot. Repeat. This new plot is a sampling distribution: its dots are sample averages, not individual people.

Why this works · a little deeper

The population plot shows individual values. The second plot shows one statistic from each sample. Its spread depends on the population, sample size, chosen statistic and sampling method. The central limit theorem often helps approximate sample means with a bell curve as sample size grows. It does not make every small or heavy-tailed sample normal. Weights, clustering and sampling much of a finite population can change the usual SE formula.

Quick check · Does a histogram of 200 sample means represent 200 people?

No. Each mean summarizes a separate sample.

What changes with sample size?

Bigger independent samples usually give steadier averages. But the improvement slows down. To halve the standard error, we need about four times as many observations under the same conditions.

Why this works · a little deeper

Increasing the sample size makes each mean steadier. Increasing the number of simulation repeats makes our picture of that distribution clearer. These are different changes. More repeats do not reduce the uncertainty of a mean calculated from a sample of the same size.

Quick check · Does increasing the number of simulation repeats reduce the SE of each mean?

No. It estimates the sampling distribution more precisely.

06Assumptions and failure modes

When is the usual SE misleading?

The usual SE formula needs independent information. Related observations can overlap in what they tell us. Very extreme values can also make small-sample approximations unreliable. More rows do not always mean equally more information.

Why this works · a little deeper

Assumptions

Repeated samples must follow the stated population and sampling design. The simple SD/√n formula assumes independent information; related observations need a suitable adjustment.

When the method fails

Repeating a sampling claim assumes the same population and method each time. A group of related observations, called a cluster, may carry less information than the same number of independent people. Skewed distributions and frequent extreme values can need larger samples or other methods. A convenience sample does not become representative just by getting bigger.

Quick check · Can a thousand correlated rows act like a thousand independent rows?

No. Their effective information can be much smaller.

07Practical workflow

Which sampling design matches the question?

Choose the group you want to describe and how people enter the sample. Random sampling helps describe that group. Random treatment assignment helps compare causes and effects. They solve different problems.

The sampling frame is the list or group that could be selected. Check who it leaves out. A random variable describes a measurement whose value is not yet known. Quick check · Does random treatment assignment make a convenience sample representative?

No. It does not change how participants entered the study.

What should a sampling result report?

Report the average, its uncertainty, sample size, and how the sample was chosen. Say who the result applies to. A very precise answer from the wrong group can still be misleading.

Next: Knowledge checks ↓
09Knowledge checks

Can I tell SD from SE?

Ask what the spread describes. Is it the spread of individual values? Use SD. Is it the spread of sample averages across repeated samples? Use SE.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Individual spreadSD
Mean-estimate spreadSE
Example: SD 15, n 25SE = 3
A sampling distribution describes…

Answer and explanation

A statistic across repeated samples. Its random object is the statistic produced by the sampling procedure.

Why does standard error often shrink with n?

Answer and explanation

Independent observations add information and averaging reduces noise. For a mean under independence, SE scales approximately as 1/√n.

Is a continuous density height a probability?

Answer and explanation

No; probability is area over an interval. A point has zero probability in a continuous model; interval area is probability.

If observations are clustered, naive SE is often…

Answer and explanation

Too small. Within-cluster similarity means less independent information than the row count suggests.

To halve a mean’s SE, n must be approximately…

Answer and explanation

Four times as large. Because SE is proportional to 1/√n, halving it requires quadrupling n.

Why this works · a little deeper

For SD 8 and n 16, estimated SE = 8/√16 = 8/4 = 2 under independent sampling. SD remains the spread of individuals. SE is the spread of the mean estimator. Always say which quantity a dot, interval or histogram describes.

Quick check · For SD = 8 and n = 16, what is SE?

8 / √16 = 2, under independent sampling.
