01Points, labels, and a decision boundary

What does a decision boundary decide?

A decision boundary separates the predicted classes. SVM gives each point a signed score. One sign predicts one class; the other sign predicts the other. A score is not a probability.

Why this works · a little deeper

A feature is an input measurement. For a linear model, score = wᵀx+b: multiply the inputs by weights and add the intercept. The boundary is score zero. A line in two inputs becomes a plane in three and a hyperplane in more. Changing score units without the fitting constraints does not create a meaningful new margin.

02Why not choose any separating line?

Why is one separator preferable to another?

Several lines may separate the same training points. SVM also considers the margin: the room around the separator. A linear SVM balances a wider margin against penalties for points that violate it.

Why this works · a little deeper

Training accuracy only counts which side each point lands on. A wider margin adds a geometric preference. Soft-margin fitting also allows points inside the margin and on the wrong side. A wide margin cannot repair data leakage, unreliable labels or an unrepresentative sample.

03The margin is protected space

What is the margin?

In the usual score scale, the two margin lines have scores +1 and −1. The distance between them is 2/‖w‖. Here ‖w‖ is the length of the model's weight vector.

Why this works · a little deeper

The score normalization places margin planes at wᵀx+b=±1. For a linear SVM, width = 2/‖w‖ in the coordinates used for fitting. Changing scaling changes that geometry. With soft margins, nearest points may lie inside or beyond these lines; the margin lines need not touch the nearest correctly classified point on each side.

04Support vectors shape the fitted separator

Why do support vectors matter?

Support vectors are training points with nonzero influence weights in the fitted SVM solution. They often sit on or inside the margin. With overlapping classes, some can be wrongly classified.

Why this works · a little deeper

In the dual formulation, α_i is a fitted weight for training row i. Rows with α_i>0 are support vectors. These can constrain the separator even when they are misclassified. Support means involvement in this fitted decision, not causal or universal importance. Small movements of safely excluded points may not change a solution, but larger changes can.

05Hard margins break when reality overlaps

Why allow violations?

Real classes can overlap, so perfect separation may be impossible or unhelpful. A soft margin allows violations and charges a penalty for them. It balances those penalties with keeping the separator simpler.

Why this works · a little deeper

Slack ξ_i measures violation of y_i score_i≥1. ξ=0 means at or beyond the correct margin; 0<ξ<1 means inside it but on the correct side. ξ=1 reaches the decision boundary; ξ>1 is on the wrong side. Using ≤1 as always correctly classified would mishandle that boundary case. The soft-margin model penalizes slack rather than requiring every row to fit perfectly.

Quick check · Can a correctly classified point still incur hinge loss?

Yes, when its signed margin is below one.

06C decides how expensive violations are

What does C penalize?

C controls how much the fit penalizes margin violations. Larger C makes those violations more expensive relative to the weight penalty. It does not promise better predictions on validation data.

Why this works · a little deeper

A common linear objective is ½‖w‖²+CΣ max(0,1−y_i score_i). Σ means add across training rows. Larger C gives violations more weight, so C is an inverse regularization setting. Its best value depends on the data, scale and objective. It is not a direct accuracy dial.

Quick check · Does doubling C double model accuracy?

No.

Why fit scaling inside a fold?

SVM geometry depends on input units. Learn any scaling inside the training fold and reuse it unchanged on validation and later rows. Scaling the test data separately changes the coordinate system.

Why this works · a little deeper

Standardization is (value−training mean)/training SD. Fit those two numbers using each training fold. Apply the same values to its held-out rows. 08Hinge loss ignores easy correct points

Why do easy correct points have zero hinge loss?

Hinge loss is max(0, 1−y×score), using labels −1 and +1. A correct point beyond the unit margin gets zero loss. A point inside the margin or on the wrong side gets a penalty.

Why this works · a little deeper

Multiply the true signed label by the score. If that product is at least 1, loss is zero. At product .4, loss is .6. At product −.3, loss is 1.3. max means take the larger of zero and the calculated amount. At score zero, a separate tie rule decides the predicted class.

09The decision score is not automatically a probability

Why is a score not a probability?

The signed score tells us which side the point occupies. Its size is measured in the separator's score units. To estimate probabilities, fit a separate calibration procedure using suitable held-out predictions.

Why this works · a little deeper

For a linear SVM, dividing score by ‖w‖ gives signed geometric distance in the fitted input scale. A kernel score is not generally literal distance in the original plotted axes. Score 2 does not mean 200% or twice the chance of score 1. Calibration maps held-out scores to estimated class probabilities under its own assumptions.

11The kernel trick avoids explicit feature explosion

What does a kernel compute?

The RBF kernel gives nearby points high similarity and far-away points low similarity. Gamma controls how quickly that similarity falls with distance. Large gamma makes influence very local.

Why this works · a little deeper

RBF similarity K(x,z)=exp(−γ‖x−z‖²). exp is the exponential function. At identical inputs the value is 1; it approaches zero with increasing distance. Small gamma spreads influence more widely, while large gamma makes it local. Gamma and C interact, and an intricate training boundary may predict poorly elsewhere.

Quick check · Does gamma have meaning independent of input scaling?

No.

How do we choose C and gamma?

Choose C, gamma, kernel and preparation together using development data. Refit learned preparation inside each training fold. Large values can make a detailed fit that follows noise rather than useful structure.
