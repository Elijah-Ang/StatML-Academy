01The Raw Data

What is the target category?

A classification tree predicts a category. It asks input questions one after another. Each final group, called a leaf, stores the training class counts. Those counts provide a label and a class fraction.

03The Root Node

Before splitting, all training rows form the root. Its most common class is a simple baseline prediction. Its class-1 fraction gives the same starting estimate to every query.

Why this works · a little deeper

If the root has 6 class-1 rows and 4 class-0 rows, its class-1 fraction is 6/10=.6. The usual .5 decision cutoff chooses class 1. That fraction describes training membership. It is not a guarantee that the next point belongs to class 1.

Quick check · What happens if every training label is 1?

The root is pure and predicts class 1.

Next: Measuring Messiness ↓
04Measuring Messiness

What is impurity?

Impurity measures how mixed the labels are in a group. For two classes, Gini is 2p(1−p), where p is the class-1 fraction. It is zero for one-class groups and 0.5 for a half-and-half mix.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
p = 0Gini 0
p = 0.5Gini 0.5
p = 1Gini 0

↳
Try it

Move the toy class proportion from 0 to 1. Compare a pure node with a half-and-half node on the Gini curve.

Why this works · a little deeper

For several classes, Gini = 1−Σp_i²; Σ means add across classes. Binary .5 is the maximum only for two equally likely classes. Entropy is another criterion; binary entropy with base-2 logs reaches 1 for an even mix. Both measure mixture, but may choose different cuts. Low impurity is not itself evidence that the features cause the labels.

Quick check · Is high impurity a desirable leaf property?

Usually not for classification fit.

Next: How the Tree Scores One Split ↓
06How the Tree Scores One Split

How is split improvement calculated?

Gain is the parent's impurity minus the children's weighted impurity. Larger child groups count more than tiny groups. A greater gain means a larger improvement under that criterion.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Parent Gini0.5
Children weighted Gini0.2
Gain0.3

Your notebook: Open the visual to inspect the current calculation.

↳
Try it

Compare the class mixtures in the two children. Weight their impurities by their sample sizes before subtracting from the parent.

Why this works · a little deeper

For child sizes n_L and n_R, weighted impurity = (n_L/n)Gini_L+(n_R/n)Gini_R. Gain subtracts this from parent impurity. Weighting avoids counting a one-row child as much as a large child. The chosen cut is locally best among allowed candidates, not an absolute best tree across all future choices.

Quick check · Can a valid improving split have negative gain?

No.

07Recursive Splitting

How is the procedure reused in a child?

After the root splits, repeat the search within each child. A branch can use a different input from another branch. Each stops when its own limits are reached.

Why this works · a little deeper

Reuse the same search inside a child rather than applying a child's cut to unrelated regions. Stop for purity, maximum depth, insufficient rows, leaf-size requirements or inadequate gain. These conditions are configurable limits. Pure training leaves do not prove future predictions are perfect.

08The Decision Boundary

Why do tree boundaries look rectangular?

A question about one input makes a horizontal or vertical cut. Several cuts make rectangular leaf regions in a two-input plot. Every point in a leaf receives that leaf's saved prediction.

Why this works · a little deeper

A leaf's class fraction is class count/leaf count. A leaf containing one positive row gives 100% locally, but that is very little evidence about future rows. Probability calibration and minimum leaf size matter. Axis-aligned cuts can represent curves through many steps, not through a guaranteed smooth boundary.

09Depth Limiting vs. Post-Pruning

How do depth limits and pruning differ?

A depth limit stops new branches being grown. Post-pruning removes branches from a tree that was already grown. Both simplify a tree, but their search paths differ.

15Limitations & Model Choice

When should I prefer another model?

A short tree can be easy to read. A deep one can be unstable or overconfident. If another model better meets the need for stability, smoothness or probability quality, compare it under the same evaluation plan.
