- heading "The Least Squares Method" [level=2]: The Least Squares Method
- paragraph: Instead of guessing, the computer uses matrix algebra to instantly derive the absolute lowest possible Sum of Squared Errors directly from the dataset.
- paragraph: By minimizing the squared residuals across all 3 dimensions simultaneously, it settles perfectly into the optimal fit.
- button "Snap to Optimal Fit"
- strong: Why Square the Errors?
- text: 1. It stops positive (+4) and negative (-4) misses from canceling out to zero.
- text: 2. It heavily punishes huge misses (a miss of 10 costs 100 points), forcing the model to compromise evenly across all variables.

- generic: Stage 07 · Understand
- heading "The Spurious Trap" [level=2]: The Spurious Trap
- paragraph:
  - text: If multiple variables just stack, why not run a simple regression for each variable separately? Because of
  - strong: Spurious Correlations
  - text: .
- paragraph:
  - text: Imagine we want to predict
  - strong: Shark Attacks
  - text: . If we run a simple model using
  - strong: Ice Cream Sales
  - text: ", the correlation is huge! It looks like ice cream causes shark attacks."
- paragraph:
  - text: "But the true driver is a lurking variable:"
  - strong: High Temperature
  - text: . Hot days cause both ice cream sales
  - emphasis: and
  - text: beach visits (which lead to shark attacks).
- strong: The Power of "Holding Constant"
- text: Multiple regression isolates a variable's
- emphasis: unique
- text: contribution, which can reveal when a variable only looked important because it moved together with another predictor. If we put both Ice Cream and Temperature into the model, the model essentially compares days with the
- emphasis: same temperature
- text: . Holding temperature constant, changing ice cream sales alone has almost zero unique effect on shark attacks.
- text: If a coefficient shrinks close to zero after the other predictors are included, that suggests it has little
- emphasis: unique
- text: relationship with the predicted outcome (y) after accounting for the others. This does
- emphasis: not
- text: prove the variable has no real-world effect at all, only that it adds little unique information within this specific model.
- paragraph:
  - emphasis: "Note: A larger coefficient doesn't always mean a \"more important\" predictor, as they depend on the unit of measurement."
- generic: Stage 08 · Reflect
- heading "The Adjusted R² Penalty" [level=2]: The Adjusted R² Penalty
- paragraph:
  - text: How do we know if our team of variables is actually good?
  - strong: R²
  - text: tells us what percentage of the variance we explained.
- paragraph:
  - text: "But raw R² has a flaw: it almost never goes down when you add predictors. Adding"
  - emphasis: any
  - text: variable—even "Student's Shoe Size"—will mathematically force raw R² to increase, tricking you into thinking the model is better.
- text: Adj R² = 1 - [ (1 - R²)(n - 1) / (n - p - 1) ]
- paragraph:
  - strong: Adjusted R²
  - text: corrects for this by penalizing model complexity. In the formula,
  - code: "n"
  - text: is the number of observations and
  - code: p
  - text: is the number of predictors.
- paragraph:
  - text: Every new predictor (
  - code: p
  - text: ) uses up flexibility and increases the penalty. A new variable should only stay if it improves the model enough to justify its cost. A useless variable will cause Adjusted R² to stay flat or drop!
- generic: Stage 09 · Evaluate
- heading "Reading the Model Output" [level=2]: Reading the Model Output
- paragraph: When a data analyst fits a multiple linear regression, they don't look at a 3D plot—they look at a summary table to evaluate the results.
- list:
  - generic: "Estimate:"
  - text: The coefficient (β). How much the prediction changes for a one-unit increase in this predictor,
  - emphasis: holding all other predictors constant
  - text: .
  - generic: "Std. Error:"
  - generic: The uncertainty around the estimate. A smaller standard error means we are more confident in the coefficient.
  - generic: "t value:"
  - generic: The estimate divided by its standard error. A larger absolute value (typically > 2) indicates strong statistical evidence that the true effect is not zero.
- paragraph: "Note: Each coefficient should be read as \"the change in predicted y for a one-unit increase in this predictor, holding the others constant.\" Because predictors can be measured in entirely different units, coefficients cannot be compared as pure \"importance scores\" unless the predictors are on comparable scales."
- strong: Diagnostics belong with the output.
- text: Inspect residual-versus-fitted, Q–Q, scale-location, leverage and influence plots. Use VIFs and coefficient intervals to diagnose multicollinearity; document categorical reference levels and interactions. Report confidence intervals for mean responses and wider prediction intervals for new observations. These coefficients are adjusted associations, not causal effects without a causal design.
- text: Multiple linear regression is a powerful detective. It models additive effects, isolates individual drivers, and finds the mathematically optimal fit by minimizing squared errors.
- generic: Complete the picture
- heading "Diagnostics the fitted equation cannot show" [level=3]
- paragraph:
  - text: Before trusting coefficient estimates, inspect residual plots and influential observations. Check
  - strong: multicollinearity
  - text: because strongly overlapping predictors can make individual coefficients unstable even when overall prediction remains good.
- paragraph: Interactions are needed when the association with one predictor changes across levels of another. Add them deliberately and interpret the combined terms rather than reading each coefficient in isolation.
- generic: Key takeaways
- list:
  - listitem: Each coefficient describes a partial relationship while the other predictors are held constant.
  - listitem: Adjusted R² penalises adding predictors that do not provide enough improvement.
  - listitem: Prediction quality, residual diagnostics, collinearity, and subject-matter logic all matter.
