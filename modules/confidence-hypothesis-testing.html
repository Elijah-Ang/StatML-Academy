01The problem

What uncertainty does an estimate leave?

An estimate is one answer from the data. An interval adds a range showing the uncertainty of that answer under a chosen method. A narrower interval means more precision. It does not mean the effect is larger.

Why this works · a little deeper

An estimate might be 4 points in both studies. If study A has SE 1 and study B has SE 3, A's interval is narrower under the same method. The interval width describes precision. Decide what size of change would matter before using the interval to judge practical value.

Quick check · Which study gives the narrower interval for the same estimate?

Study A, because its standard error is smaller.

02Intuition and objective

What do a confidence interval and a p-value ask?

A confidence interval is made by a method designed to cover the fixed population value at a stated rate across repeated samples. A p-value asks how unusual the data would be under a chosen null model. Neither gives the chance that the null model is true.

Why this works · a little deeper

A 95% confidence method covers the fixed parameter in 95% of repeated uses under its assumptions. Once this interval is calculated, the parameter is not randomly moving around inside it. For the usual frequentist interval, do not call 95% the probability that this fixed parameter lies in this specific interval. The p-value assumes the null and asks about results at least as extreme.

Quick check · Does a 95% interval assign 95% probability to this fixed parameter?

Not in the usual frequentist interpretation.

03Worked example

Worked example

How do we compute one interval and test?

For independent normal measurements with known population SD, SE = SD/√n. A 95% interval is estimate ± 1.96×SE. Compare the estimate with the null value using z = (estimate − null)/SE. If SD is estimated, a t reference is usually needed instead.

04Reasoning with the method

Which areas are false alarms, power, and missed detection?

Alpha is the planned false-alarm rate when the null model is true. Power is the chance of detecting a particular real effect under the planned method. Bigger samples, bigger effects and less noise usually make detection easier.

Why this works · a little deeper

A Type I error rejects a true null; alpha controls its rate under the assumptions. A Type II error misses a specific alternative; beta is its probability. Power = 1−beta. Lowering alpha usually lowers power if everything else stays the same. Family-wise control limits the chance of any false rejection in a family; false-discovery-rate control targets the expected proportion of false discoveries. These are different goals. Neither automatically repairs outcome switching or unplanned stopping.

Quick check · Is power the probability that an observed result is true?

No. Power is a planned chance of detecting one specified effect.

05Interactive laboratory

Why does larger n change precision?

With the same spread, a bigger independent sample gives smaller SE and a narrower interval. It can make a tiny effect easier to detect. The effect itself has not become more useful just because the interval narrowed.

Quick check · Can an enormous study detect a trivial effect?

Yes. Statistical precision and practical importance are separate.

06Assumptions and failure modes

Which areas are false alarms, power, and missed detection?

Related observations, many tests, unplanned stopping and a poor model can break the promised error rate. Deciding the analysis after seeing the answer also changes what the reported p-value means.

Why this works · a little deeper

Assumptions

The estimator, test and sampling design must fit the question. Stated error rates rely on their model assumptions and a suitable plan for repeated comparisons.

When the method fails

Dependence means some observations share information. Multiple testing means many opportunities for a small p-value. Optional stopping means repeatedly checking and stopping by an unplanned rule. Each issue needs its own suitable design or analysis. A multiple-testing adjustment does not fix wrongly treating related rows as independent.

Quick check · Does correcting for many tests fix wrongly treating related rows as independent?

No. Multiple testing and related observations are different problems. Each needs its own suitable response.

No. That is outcome selection and needs explicit exploratory treatment.

08Interpretation and reporting

How do we report a result without overclaiming?

Report the estimate and interval, as well as the test method and p-value. If a test does not reject zero, the evidence was not enough for that test. It does not prove the effect is exactly zero.

Why this works · a little deeper

What to report: Give the estimate in meaningful units, uncertainty interval, sample size, method, assumptions and any multiple-comparison plan. A wide interval containing zero can also contain worthwhile effects. To show an effect is small enough to count as equivalent, define an equivalence margin and use an appropriate planned analysis.

Quick check · Can a wide interval including zero prove equivalence?

No. Equivalence needs a planned margin and an appropriate analysis.

09Knowledge checks

Can I interpret uncertainty correctly?

Read the size and uncertainty together. An estimate can be small and precise, large and uncertain, or too uncertain to decide. A yes/no significance label leaves out these differences.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Estimate0.2 units
95% interval[0.1, 0.3]
Meaningful effect2 units
A 95% confidence procedure means…

Answer and explanation

Across repeated samples, the method covers the fixed population value 95% of the time under its assumptions. The percentage describes the repeated method, not a probability assigned to this fixed value after seeing one interval.

A p-value is not…

Answer and explanation

The probability the null is true. The calculation assumes the null model and the test assumptions. It does not calculate the chance that the null is true.

Testing 20 outcomes requires attention to…

Answer and explanation

Extra false-alarm chances from testing many outcomes. Multiple opportunities for small p-values inflate false positives.

Failing to reject means…

Answer and explanation

Evidence was insufficient at the chosen rule. Absence of sufficient evidence is not evidence of exact absence.

What completes an inferential result?

Answer and explanation

Size of the effect, uncertainty range and practical meaning. Explain how big the estimate is and how uncertain it is, then compare it with a meaningful difference.
