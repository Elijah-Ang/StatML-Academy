01What problem is One-R solving?

Which part of a new row does One-R actually read?

One-R predicts a class using just one input column. Each category or numeric interval gets its own saved prediction. This makes it a simple baseline we can inspect and explain.

Why this works · a little deeper

A feature is an input column; the target is the answer to predict. In a separate 14-day umbrella example, candidate inputs are outlook, humidity, wind and temperature. One-R uses one of them at prediction time. It is a baseline, not a claim that one feature explains the whole outcome.

Why this works · a little deeper

For Outlook=Sunny, collect those training rows, count Yes and No, then save the more common answer. Repeat for Cloudy and Rainy. Errors for a value are rows disagreeing with its saved class. 06Missing values and unseen categories are part of the model.

What happens when the required input is missing?

Save a policy for missing values, new categories and ties. For example, use a learned missing-value group or the overall training majority. Do not invent a new rule after seeing an evaluation answer.

If Outlook wins, a Cloudy row may look up Yes while humidity and wind are ignored. That means Outlook gave the best single-column fit among the candidates under this procedure. 08One feature cannot express an interaction.

Can one coordinate describe a two-coordinate ring pattern?

One input alone can miss a pattern that needs two inputs together. That combined dependence is an interaction. A simple, readable rule can still miss useful structure.

Why this works · a little deeper

XOR is a simple interaction: the class is positive when exactly one of two binary inputs is 1. Each input alone may tell little, while the pair determines the answer. A one-column lookup cannot capture that full rule. An interpretable feature can also be a proxy for another factor; readability is not evidence of causality or suitability.

09Validate the whole learning process

What must be refitted inside each validation fold?

Inside each training fold, relearn all data-based choices: intervals, value rules, fallback and selected input. Then score the held-out rows. Selecting a feature before the folds would use their answers too early.

Why this works · a little deeper

For every fold, learn preparation and rules on the training part, freeze them, then predict the held-out part. Keep repeated people, devices or organizations together when the deployment question requires new groups. Otherwise the model may recognize a group rather than generalize to a genuinely new one.

Why this works · a little deeper

Precision asks how often alerts are correct; recall asks how many actual positives are found. Report fold scores, their average and spread, and compare the baseline on the same splits. Fold models share training rows, so their SD is not automatically a confidence interval. There are no universal percentage bands that say a model is stable enough to deploy.

11Use simple models as diagnostic instruments.

How does a three-interval rule differ from a one-split rule?

A decision stump makes one split and has two leaves. One-R can make several rules for different values or intervals of one input. Both are simple, but they divide the data differently.

Why this works · a little deeper

One-R uses one feature but may map many of its values to different classes. A numeric stump makes one cutoff; categorical stump behavior depends on the split implementation. A full tree can make later questions depend on earlier answers. If one feature nearly matches a complex model, check useful signal and possible leakage rather than assuming either explanation.
