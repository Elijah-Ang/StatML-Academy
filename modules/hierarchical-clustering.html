What does grouping without an answer key mean?

Clustering groups cases without known correct labels. Agglomerative clustering starts with each point in its own group. It repeatedly joins the closest two groups under the chosen rule.

Why this works · a little deeper

Unsupervised means the grouping is not trained against an answer label. A cluster is a group made by the selected distance and linkage. There may be several useful groupings, or no meaningful grouping for the task. Do not promise that the algorithm discovers one hidden true set of categories.

Quick check · Does the algorithm discover uniquely true categories?

No. Groups depend on the data, distance, and linkage.

Why this works · a little deeper

Euclidean distance is straight-line distance in the chosen coordinates. Other options include Manhattan, cosine-related or domain-specific measures. Scaling can change which pair is nearest. Choose it according to units and the purpose; automatic standardization is not always desirable. For deployment or validation tasks, any learned preparation needs an appropriate fitting boundary.

Why this works · a little deeper

The smallest eligible pairwise value gives the first merge. If several pairs tie, a fixed implementation rule chooses their order. The next merge is chosen using updated group distances, not simply the next original point-to-point distance.

04Defining Group Distance (Linkage)

How far apart are groups?

Linkage tells us how to compare groups. Single linkage uses the nearest pair; complete uses the farthest. Average uses the mean pairwise distance. Ward uses an increase in within-group squared spread.

Why this works · a little deeper

Point distance and group linkage are different choices. Single linkage can form chains; complete often favors compact groups. Ward is tied to Euclidean squared geometry and minimizes an increase in within-cluster spread. Dendrogram height does not mean the same mathematical quantity for every linkage, so label the method and scale.

05The Process in Motion

How is the merge history built?

Every merge reduces the number of groups by one. The method records the history without undoing earlier merges. An early decision can therefore affect the whole tree.

Why this works · a little deeper

After one merge, replace the two old groups by their combined group and update linkage distances. Repeat. A complete n-point agglomerative tree has n−1 merges and ends in one group. A display may stop earlier at a chosen number. The procedure is greedy and does not reconsider previous merges.

Quick check · How many merges turn eight singletons into three groups?

Five.

06The Family Tree (Dendrogram)

What does dendrogram height represent?

A dendrogram is a picture of that merge history. Its merge height shows the linkage value. The horizontal spacing of leaves is for layout; it is not a distance scale between nearby points.

Why this works · a little deeper

Read upwards from the original leaves. Each join names the groups merged and the linkage value then used. Adjacent leaves may be far apart in the original data; their left-right order is a drawing choice. Rotate a branch without changing its merges and the clustering remains the same.

Quick check · Does a wider horizontal gap between leaves mean greater distance?

No.

07Cutting the Tree

How does a horizontal cut produce K groups?

Cut the tree at a height to keep the groups joined below that line. Or stop after n−K merges to get K groups. Tied heights can make choosing only by height ambiguous.

Why this works · a little deeper

A horizontal cut determines connected groups below it. Large jumps in height can suggest candidate cuts, but do not prove natural categories. Compare stability and suitable measures such as silhouette, with attention to the distance and task. n−K merges fixes a count even when equal heights make a height-only rule less clear.

Quick check · Does requesting K refit the entire merge history?

No. It selects a partition from the same hierarchy.

09Synthesis & Recap

When is this preferable to K-Means?

Hierarchical clustering gives a nested history and lets us choose K after fitting. It can be expensive on large datasets. Assigning future points usually needs an extra rule.

Why this works · a little deeper

K-means asks for K before fitting and returns one flat partition. Hierarchical clustering returns a merge tree and allows later cuts. Runtime depends on implementation and data size; neither is universally faster or more meaningful. A standard fitted hierarchy does not automatically give a prediction rule for a new point. Report distance, linkage, scaling, cut and stability evidence.

Quick check · Is cutting a dendrogram equivalent to K-Means?

No. Their objectives and fitting procedures differ.
