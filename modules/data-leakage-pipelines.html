01The problem

How can a good score be misleading?

A model can get a great score by seeing clues it would not have at prediction time. This is data leakage. Ask when each input becomes known and which rows influenced each choice.

Why this works · a little deeper

Prediction time is the moment the model must decide. A clue recorded after the outcome, such as a later billing code, may reveal the answer without being available then. A score using that clue evaluates a procedure we cannot actually use at the intended time.

Quick check · Is a discharge diagnosis valid for an admission-time prediction?

Usually not. It contains later information.

02Intuition and objective

What information is allowed to cross a split?

Learn preparation from training rows, then apply those saved values to other rows. Validation or test information must not quietly help create the fitted procedure.

Why this works · a little deeper

The pipeline includes everything that learns from data, not only the final classifier. Means, missing-value fills, category frequencies, selected inputs, PCA directions, oversampling rules and thresholds can all carry information. Fit them within the relevant training or development boundary. An untouched outer evaluation must stay outside every such choice.

Why this works · a little deeper

For 1,000 rows in five equal folds, each turn has 800 training and 200 validation rows. If we fill missing ages with the mean of all 1,000 first, every validation group helps set its own fill. Instead fit five training-only imputers, one for each turn, then apply each to its 200 held-out rows.

Quick check · Does this imply a universal fixed score inflation?

No. The performance effect depends on the data and task.

04Reasoning with the method

What kinds of leakage exist?

Information can leak through inputs, duplicate rows, repeated people, future dates or repeated model choices. A correctly ordered code pipeline cannot fix a split that answers the wrong prediction question.

Why this works · a little deeper

Duplicate images across folds leak row information. Repeated people may leak identity. A rolling feature using future values leaks time. Repeatedly choosing from final-test scores leaks selection information. Target encoding needs cross-fitting so a training row's own target does not create its encoded input. Correct software order still needs the right groups, times and deployment question.

Quick check · Are duplicate images split across folds independent evidence?

No.

Can I repair this pipeline?

Split first. Fit learned preparation on training rows. Apply it to held-out rows without relearning it. Fit the model and score the predictions. Repeat fitting within every validation fold.

fit means learn parameters from the training part. transform means apply those learned parameters to another part. Do not fit a separate validation scaler or use validation labels to choose missing-value fills. A prediction should be made before its held-out outcome is used for scoring.

06Assumptions and failure modes

What can pipelines fail to prevent?

A pipeline cannot make a future clue available earlier. It cannot turn related observations into independent cases. Check where the data came from and what would be known during real use.

Why this works · a little deeper

Assumptions

The split must match prediction time and the independent unit. Each input must exist when a decision is made. Learned preparation must stay inside training.

When the method fails

Trace source dates, measurement availability, duplicates and related groups. Random row folds may be unsuitable for forecasting or prediction for new people or organizations. A pipeline protects its declared boundaries; it cannot decide the right independent unit on its own.

07Practical workflow

How should nested selection work?

Nested validation puts tuning inside another evaluation loop. The inner loop selects settings. The outer held-out rows check the whole selection process and must not influence inner choices.

Why this works · a little deeper

For each outer split, reserve its evaluation rows. On the remaining rows, fit and compare candidates using inner folds. Refit the selected procedure on the outer training portion, then predict the untouched outer rows. This assesses a selection method, rather than just one candidate's favorable inner score.

No.

Can I spot a new leakage path?

Trace the information in a new example. Find where a held-out answer could affect a value or decision. Repair that path instead of merely trying to lower the suspicious score.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
ScenarioVisits from one patient in both sets
Leakage pathPatient-specific information
RepairKeep patient groups together
Which workflow leaks?

Answer and explanation

Scale all rows, then cross-validate. Scaling all rows lets each validation fold influence its own transformation.

Where should oversampling happen?

Answer and explanation

Inside each training fold. Oversampling must not create synthetic or duplicated information across held-out boundaries.

Repeated visits from each patient require…

Answer and explanation

Cross-validation keeping each person’s visits together. All visits from one patient belong in the same fold.

For forecasting, use…

Answer and explanation

Time-ordered validation. Every validation observation must occur after the observations used to fit it.

What is target leakage?

Answer and explanation

Using information unavailable at prediction time that reveals the outcome. Availability at the real prediction moment is the key test.
