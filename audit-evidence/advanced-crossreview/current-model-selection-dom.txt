- generic: Stage 01 · Build
- heading "The Overfitting Trap" [level=2]: The Overfitting Trap
- generic: Before you start
- paragraph: Model selection is part of model fitting. Every candidate comparison must be contained inside the training or cross-validation process.
- paragraph: Building a predictive model is tricky. It is tempting to include every available piece of data. We often assume that more clues guarantee better answers.
- paragraph: However, adding too many variables introduces massive noise. This noise obscures the true relationship. The model ends up memorizing random accidents in the training data rather than learning the actual signal.
- paragraph: "The Illusion of Accuracy: A highly complex model performs flawlessly on its \"homework\" (the training data). But it collapses in the real world on unseen data. This failure is called overfitting."

- heading "The Shrinking Trick" [level=2]: The Shrinking Trick
- paragraph: Subset selection is an "all-or-nothing" approach. A variable is either in or out. But what if we just made a weak variable's influence smaller?
- paragraph:
  - text: This is called Regularization. Imagine a soundboard where each slider controls a variable. Regularization turns down the volume on loud, messy variables. It shrinks their coefficients (
  - generic: β
  - math:
    - generic: β
  - text: ) toward zero using a tuning dial called
  - generic: λ

- paragraph: "Crucial Step: Variables should be centered first, and usually standardized when their scales differ, to create a level playing field. Otherwise, a variable measured in huge numbers (like dollars) would face unfair shrinkage compared to a variable measured in small numbers (like years)."

- generic: Stage 09 · Understand
- heading "The \"Super-Variable\" Secret" [level=2]: The "Super-Variable" Secret
- paragraph: If we have 100 highly correlated variables, they provide redundant information. Dimension Reduction allows us to "squish" them into a few super-variables called Principal Components.
- heading "PCR" [level=3]
- paragraph: Compress first, predict later. It squishes variables together without looking at what you are trying to predict.
- heading "PLS" [level=3]
- paragraph: Compress while paying attention. It squishes variables together by finding directions that best explain the target you want to predict.
- paragraph: Think of PCR as combining 100 photos of a dog into a single "Super-Photo" of the whole dog. PLS knows you are trying to predict happiness, so it squishes the photos specifically to highlight the wagging tail.

- heading "Lasso & Best Subset Selection" [level=3]
- paragraph: Best for Interpretability & Simplicity. These methods drop variables entirely, leaving a clean, short list of the most important drivers. Essential in healthcare or finance where explaining the "why" matters.
- heading "Ridge & PCR" [level=3]
- paragraph: Best for Pure Prediction & Correlated Data. When you have many overlapping clues and prediction accuracy matters more than a simple explanation, keeping all variables with smaller, stable weights helps minimize error on new data.
- generic: Stage 11 · Reflect
- heading "Common Mistakes" [level=2]: Common Mistakes
- paragraph: Even advanced statistical methods are not magic. Here is how to avoid common traps.
- paragraph: Mistake 1
- paragraph: Choosing a model based on training accuracy.
- paragraph: Correct Practice
- paragraph: Always compare and judge models based on cross-validation error. Training accuracy is always overly optimistic.
- paragraph: Mistake 2
- paragraph: Testing on the final test set multiple times.
- paragraph: Correct Practice
- paragraph: Lock the final test set in a vault. Use it only once at the very end to check your final model. If you use it to tweak parameters, it is no longer an honest test.
- generic: Complete the picture
- heading "Prefer the simplest near-best model" [level=3]
- paragraph:
  - text: Validation scores contain noise. The
  - strong: one-standard-error rule
  - text: chooses the simplest model whose performance is within one standard error of the best observed score.
- paragraph: When comparing many pipelines, use nested cross-validation or a final untouched test set. Otherwise the “best” model may simply be the one that benefited most from repeated checking.
- generic: Stage 12 · Reflect
- heading "Final Synthesis" [level=2]: Final Synthesis
- paragraph: We do not want a model that is too simple (underfitting). We do not want a model that is too dramatic (overfitting).
- paragraph: By using Cross-Validation, we compare and validate models fairly. By applying Regularization or Subset Selection, we penalize messiness and force the model to focus only on what matters.
- paragraph: The ultimate goal is Generalization. We want to build a model that stays trustworthy and useful when reality shows up and starts ruining everyone's assumptions.
