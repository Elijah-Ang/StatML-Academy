01The Raw Data & The Question

Why does row order matter?

A time series is a list of measurements in time order. Nearby measurements may be related. When checking a forecast, only use information that was available when that forecast would have been made.

02The Time Plot

What should I notice before modeling?

Plot the values against time first. Look for a long-term rise or fall, repeating seasons, unusual events and changing spread. These clues help choose a model. They do not promise that the pattern will continue.

How do components add up?

Quick check · Must seasonal amplitude stay fixed in every time-series model?

No. A multiplicative or transformed model can represent changing amplitude.

04The Assumption of Stationarity

What stays stable in a stationary process?

A weakly stationary process has a stable average and finite, stable variance. The relationship between observations depends on their time gap rather than their dates. A flat-looking plot alone cannot prove this.

Why this works · a little deeper

Variance measures squared spread around the average. Autocovariance measures how two time-separated values vary together. Weak stationarity requires that relationship to depend on the lag, not the calendar date. Removing a trend does not guarantee stable variance, seasonality or dependence. A short sample plot is evidence to inspect, not proof of the full process assumptions.

Quick check · Does removing a trend automatically establish stationarity?

No. Variance, seasonality, or dependence may still change.

05Fixing It: Differencing

What does differencing actually subtract?

First differencing means subtracting the previous value. Seasonal differencing means subtracting the value one full season earlier. This can remove some changes, but it is not a cure for every pattern.

06Looking to the Past (Lags & ACF)

What is a lag?

A lag is a time gap. Autocorrelation compares a series with an earlier copy of itself. For monthly data, lag 12 compares a month with the same month a year earlier.

Why this works · a little deeper

At lag k, pair values k time steps apart. The displayed ACF adds products of their gaps from the mean and scales by the full series' squared gaps. ACF means autocorrelation function. Trend and seasonality can affect it. A large lag-12 value is a pattern to investigate, not evidence of a causal annual mechanism. This plot does not show significance bands.

Quick check · Does a high lag-12 ACF by itself prove a causal annual mechanism?

No. It is a descriptive pattern requiring context.

08Diagnostics: Evaluating Residuals

What patterns should residuals not retain?

A forecast residual is actual value minus predicted value. Look for remaining trends, waves, changing spread or an average error away from zero. A model may have missed something predictable.

Why this works · a little deeper

Small random residual patterns can occur even with a suitable model. Large repeated structure suggests information the model has not captured. Forecast errors may also have horizon-dependent variance, so constant spread is not a universal requirement. Examine the model's actual error assumptions and the forecast horizon rather than demanding that every sample correlation equal zero.

Quick check · Must every sample residual autocorrelation be exactly zero?

No. Small random values occur even under an adequate model.

10Limitations & Caveats

When might yesterday stop predicting tomorrow?

The process can change. New policies, habits, sensors or seasons may make old patterns less useful. Watch both inputs and prediction errors. Investigate a change before assuming retraining is the answer.

Why this works · a little deeper

Changes in a process are called structural breaks or drift. Historical fit cannot guarantee performance after an unprecedented change. A change in inputs is a warning, not proof that predictions fail. Inspect later outcomes, errors and data quality. Uncertainty may grow with the horizon, but the exact pattern depends on the forecasting model.

Quick check · Does input drift alone prove prediction failure?

No. It is a warning to examine outcomes and model performance.
