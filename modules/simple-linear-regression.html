Why this works · a little deeper

A row is one measured student. An input is a clue available when we predict; the target is the score we want to predict. The population mean model is E[y|x]=β₀+β₁x, meaning average y at a given x. Individual scores vary around it. Here all five rows are used to show training arithmetic. A predictive claim needs separate evaluation data. Other factors, such as prior preparation, could affect both hours and scores.

Quick check · If two students study for three hours, must their scores match?

No. The line estimates a mean response. Individual scores can differ because of other influences and random variation.

02Make your guess

What do slope and intercept actually do?

Slope controls the line's tilt. It tells us the predicted score change for one extra study hour. Intercept moves the line up or down. It is the prediction at zero hours.

ŷ = b + mx

Try it

Change only the slope. Then change only the intercept. Watch which movement each controls.

Why this works · a little deeper

ŷ means predicted y; the small hat marks an estimate. In ŷ=b+mx, m is slope and b intercept. Slope has score-points per hour; intercept has score-points. Negative slope is allowed. If zero hours is outside the data, the intercept may lack a useful real-world interpretation. The trial line is a choice to inspect, not necessarily the fitted best line.

Quick check · With slope 7, what does two more hours change in the prediction?

The prediction rises by 14 score points. This is a model-based association, not a guaranteed individual improvement.

Next: Measuring the mistakes ↓
03Measuring the mistakes

How wrong is one prediction?

A residual is actual score minus predicted score. It is the vertical gap from the line to the dot. Above the line means a positive gap; below means a negative one.

Why this works · a little deeper

For actual 61 and predicted 65, residual is −4: the prediction was four points too high. A residual +4 means four points too low. Least squares measures vertical outcome gaps at fixed x. It does not minimize the shortest diagonal distance to the line, which is a different fitting problem. Residuals are observed fitting errors, not the unobserved population disturbances themselves.

Quick check · The actual score is 61 and the predicted score is 65. What is the residual?

61 − 65 = −4. The model overpredicted by four points.

Next: A penalty that cannot cancel ↓
04A penalty that cannot cancel

Why square the errors?

Errors of +4 and −4 add to zero, hiding two mistakes. Squaring means multiplying each error by itself. Both now contribute 16. Large errors get much larger penalties.

+4 and −4016 + 16 = 32

Current SSE: 51.0 score-points².
Current MAE: 2.6 score points.

Try it

Compare each signed error with its square. Add the five squared values and check SSE. Why does a negative error still increase this total?

Why this works · a little deeper

SSE means sum of squared errors: add every (actual−predicted)². Σ means add over rows. MAE instead averages error sizes without squaring; it uses a different fitting objective and is less dominated by large misses. The drawing's square tile area represents squared error, not another input coordinate. SSE has squared score units; RMSE and residual standard error return to score units.

Quick check · Which contributes more to SSE: one residual of 8 or two residuals of 4?

The residual of 8 contributes 64. Two residuals of 4 contribute 16 + 16 = 32.

Next: The least-squares engine ↓
05The least-squares engine

How does the best line get its numbers?

Find average hours and average score. Use how they vary together and the spread of hours to calculate the slope. Then set the intercept so the fitted line goes through those two averages.

Try it

Fit the line, then edit one observation. The calculation receipt updates from those same rows.

Why this works · a little deeper

The slope is Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ)/Σ(x−x̄)². Bars mark averages. The intercept is ȳ−m x̄. These formulas solve the least-squares equations directly, without iterative training. If all x values are identical, the bottom is zero: we can estimate the mean there but cannot uniquely separate slope and intercept. When x varies, this fit gives the unique minimum within this line family.

Quick check · Why can’t we learn a slope if every student has exactly three study hours?

There is no variation in x to compare. Many slope/intercept pairs give the same prediction at x = 3.

Next: The optimal line ↓
06The optimal line

What does “best” promise?

Best means the smallest training sum of squared errors among straight lines with an intercept. It does not mean the model found a cause or will predict every future student well.

Why this works · a little deeper

With an intercept, fitted residuals sum to zero up to rounding, and the line passes through the average x and y. Those are algebraic properties, not proof that assumptions or data quality are good. A tiny training SSE can coexist with bias, a curved true pattern or poor predictions outside training. Compare suitable new data and inspect what the residuals missed.

Quick check · Can another straight line have a smaller training SSE than this fit?

Not when the same rows, squared-error objective and intercept are used and x varies. Another model family or objective is a different comparison.

Next: The model scorecard ↓
07The model scorecard

Better than what—and by how much?

The baseline predicts the same average score for everybody. R² compares the line's squared errors with that baseline. Residual standard error estimates the remaining error spread in score units.

Why this works · a little deeper

For n rows, residual standard error uses √[SSE/(n−2)] because two coefficients were fitted. It needs positive residual degrees of freedom. Training R² with an intercept is between 0 and 1 when outcomes vary; held-out R² can be negative if the model is worse than its comparison baseline. Constant outcomes make this definition undefined. Mean-response intervals describe an estimated average; prediction intervals also include individual variation. Neither interval is drawn in this lab.

Quick check · Does an R² of 0.99 prove that study hours cause exam performance?

No. It describes a strong fit on these observations. Confounding, selection and future prediction remain separate questions.

08Predict with care

When is the line being asked to guess too far?

Interpolation predicts inside the measured range of hours. Extrapolation predicts outside it. The line can return a number anywhere, but distant predictions may have little evidence behind them.

Why this works · a little deeper

The original x range is 1–5. A prediction at 4 is interpolation; at 20 it is distant extrapolation. Define prediction time, fit on training, inspect errors and influential points, compare a baseline on validation, then lock the procedure before final testing. This lab's line and scores use the five displayed rows and are training quantities. A fitted association does not establish causation.

Quick check · Is a prediction at x = 4 or x = 20 better supported by these original observations?

x = 4. The original observed range is 1–5. x = 20 is a distant extrapolation.
