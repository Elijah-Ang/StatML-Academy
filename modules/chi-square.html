Chi-Square Test of Independence

01The Raw Counts

Why use counts instead of averages?

Use counts when the values are categories, such as club and favourite snack. Put each person in the matching table cell. Chi-square compares those real counts with counts expected if the two categories were unrelated.

02Hypotheses & Marginals

What does independence predict?

Independence means knowing the row category would not change the outcome proportions in the population. The expected table keeps the same row totals and column totals. It spreads the outcomes across rows at the overall rates.

Why this works · a little deeper

The null model says club and snack are independent in the population. The alternative says they are associated. Row and column totals are called margins. Independence does not require equally sized rows. It requires the same population snack proportions across club categories, after accounting for their sizes.

Quick check · Does independence require equal row sizes?

No. Expected counts account for unequal margins.

03Expected Counts

Why multiply row and column totals?

Take the row's size and multiply it by the overall fraction in the column. That is the expected count. For 40 people in a row and 60% overall recovery, we expect 40 × 0.60 = 24 recoveries.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Column proportion60 / 100
Expected count40 × 60 / 100 = 24

Why this works · a little deeper

Expected = row total × column total ÷ grand total. In the separate club example, 30 students are in Art and 25 of all 50 prefer Apples. Expected Art/Apples = 30 × 25/50 = 15. Art/Chips is also 15. The 20-person Sports row has expected counts 10 and 10. Expected is an average under a model; real sample counts need not match it exactly, even when the model is true.

Quick check · With row total 50 and column share 30%, what is expected?

15 observations.

04Residuals & Contributions

Why square and divide by expected count?

For each cell, subtract expected from observed. Multiply the gap by itself, then divide by expected. Add all the cell results. Squaring stops positive and negative gaps cancelling each other.

Expected24
Contribution(30 − 24)² / 24 = 1.5

Why this works · a little deeper

χ² = Σ[(O − E)²/E]. O is observed, E is expected, and Σ means add across cells. For O = 20 and E = 15, the gap is 5, its square is 25, and 25/15 is about 1.67. The separate club table's four contributions add to about 8.33. Rounded displays may not add exactly; the calculation uses unrounded values. Contributions are not negative and do not show the direction of a gap.

Quick check · Can a cell contribution be negative?

No. Every squared contribution is nonnegative.

05The Reference Distribution

How unusual is the statistic under independence?

The p-value is the area to the right of our statistic on the reference curve. It tells us how often a statistic at least this large would occur under independence and the test assumptions. It is not the chance that independence is true.

Why this works · a little deeper

For r rows and c columns, df = (r − 1)(c − 1). A 2×2 table has df 1: once the margins and one cell are fixed, the other cells follow. With df 1, the usual 5% cutoff is about 3.84. A statistic beyond it is evidence against the independence model under the assumptions; it does not rule out chance or prove causation.

Quick check · Is the p-value the chance the variables are independent?

No. Independence is assumed when computing the tail probability.

06Reading the Output

What can I conclude from the output?

A small p-value gives evidence against independence under the test model. It does not tell us the cause or how large the link is. Look at cell proportions and an association-size measure too.

Why this works · a little deeper

For observed [20,10;5,15], expected is [15,15;10,10]. N = 50, χ² ≈ 8.333, df = 1, and p ≈ 0.00389. Cramér's V = √(8.333/50) ≈ 0.408. V describes association size; labels such as moderate depend on context. A positive O − E means more than expected; a negative one means fewer. These describe an association, not what caused it.

Quick check · Does chi-square tell us which variable causes the other?

No. Causality depends on design and additional assumptions.

Next: Assumptions & Caveats ↓
07Assumptions & Caveats

When is the approximation unsuitable?

Tiny expected counts can make the usual approximation unreliable. Related people, repeat visits and survey weights can also require a different method. Choose a test that matches how the data was collected.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Sparse 2×2 countsConsider an exact test

Why this works · a little deeper

Use mutually exclusive categories and observations appropriate for the independence model. Several visits from one person are not several independent people. The usual chi-square reference can work poorly with small expected counts. Fisher's exact test is an option for sparse 2×2 tables; larger tables may need other exact or simulation methods. Yates' correction is specific to 2×2 tables and does not guarantee sparse-data reliability. State the counts, method, assumptions, effect size and pattern without a causal claim.

Quick check · Are 100 visits by ten people 100 independent people?

No. The repeated observations violate the usual assumption that these observations are independent.
