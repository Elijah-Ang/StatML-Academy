Why might a straight line miss the pattern?

A straight line can miss a curved pattern. Polynomial regression adds powers of the input so the predicted average can bend. It still combines its coefficients by multiplying and adding.

Why this works · a little deeper

The model is linear in its coefficients even when its graph curves in x. For degree 2, ŷ=b₀+b₁x+b₂x². A valley or hill may be easier to represent than with one line. A polynomial is one possible curve family, not the only option.

Why this works · a little deeper

Underfitting means the model misses important structure because its allowed shape is too restricted. A straight line can be a useful baseline even when it misses a curve. Compare errors on the same observations and evaluation plan. A poor baseline does not prove any more complex model will succeed.

Quick check · Is a complicated curve useful if validation error rises?

Usually not for this predictive task.

03Expand the Features

Why create x-squared and x-cubed?

Make extra columns from the same input, such as x² and x³. x² means x×x; x³ means x×x×x. These columns allow new shapes. They do not give us extra observed rows.

Why this works · a little deeper

For x=3, the new columns are x=3, x²=9 and x³=27. The rows still refer to the same observations. A design matrix is the table of these model columns. Scaling and centring can improve numerical stability before building powers. Learn any such transformation on training data only.

Quick check · Does creating five powers multiply the sample size by five?

No. The number of observations is unchanged.

04Fit the Weights

What do the weights do?

Each coefficient controls one term in the sum. Together, the terms make the curve. Do not read one polynomial coefficient as the whole effect of x; the terms work together.

Why this works · a little deeper

The prediction adds all basis terms. A basis function is an ingredient such as x². A one-unit change in x changes several ingredients at once, so b₂ alone is not a constant overall slope. Closely related powers can make coefficient estimates unstable. Inspect the combined prediction and uncertainty.

Quick check · Should a quadratic coefficient be interpreted without the other terms?

No. Inspect the combined curve and its domain.

05Compute Errors

What do residual patterns tell us?

Residuals are actual values minus the curve's predictions. A repeated bend in those errors can mean the curve missed a pattern. A widening funnel can mean the error spread changes.

Why this works · a little deeper

Residuals reveal what the fitted mean did not capture. A curve suggests a missing mean pattern; a funnel suggests unequal error variance. Neither shape identifies the cause by itself. Low training MSE does not prove that predictions on new data are reliable.

Quick check · Does random-looking residual scatter prove causal validity?

No. It checks only some aspects of the fitted model.

06Check for Overfitting

Why can a flexible curve do worse on new data?

A flexible curve can follow noise in the training points. It may then predict badly on other points, especially near or beyond the edges. Use validation data to check whether the extra bends help.

Why this works · a little deeper

Adding many degrees can make training SSE small while validation error grows. That is one sign of overfitting. Real error curves can be irregular rather than a perfect U-shape. A polynomial can change dramatically outside the measured range, called extrapolation. The live gold validation points are for development; they are not an untouched final test.

Quick check · Why is extrapolation especially risky for high-degree polynomials?

Powers can grow rapidly outside the observed range.
