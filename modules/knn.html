01The Raw Data

What is stored by KNN?

KNN keeps examples with known labels. For a new point, it finds the K nearest training examples and uses their labels to predict. K is the number of neighbours allowed to vote.

Why this works · a little deeper

A label is the known answer for a training row. A query is the new point we want to label. In a simple fruit example, three neighbours labelled Apple, Apple and Orange give two Apple votes and one Orange vote. KNN predicts Apple. It stores examples rather than fitting a coefficient equation. It still relies on useful measurements, distance and representative training coverage.

Training examples are the neighbours we search. Validation examples help choose K and the distance rule. Final-test examples check the finished choices. Keep those roles separate.

04Feature Scaling

Why do units affect neighbors?

Distance uses the numbers as given. A range of thousands can dominate a range of single digits. Scaling can put features on comparable ranges. Learn that scaling from training data, then reuse it unchanged.

Why this works · a little deeper

Min-max scaling is (value−training minimum)/(training maximum−training minimum). With value 7, minimum 2 and maximum 9, that is 5/7≈.71. New values may fall outside 0–1; do not secretly refit the scale on them. Scaling is a choice about what near means, not a guarantee of equal feature importance. If units already have a useful shared meaning, automatic rescaling may be unwanted.

Next: Measuring Distance ↓
05Measuring Distance

How do input gaps determine the nearest neighbors?

For straight-line distance, find the gap on each axis, square each gap, add them, then take the square root. This is Euclidean distance. With scaled inputs, use the scaled gaps.

Why this works · a little deeper

For query (.45,.55) and point (.50,.65), the gaps are .05 and .10. Distance = √(.05²+.10²)≈.1118. Squaring means multiply by itself; a square root reverses that operation for a nonnegative value. Manhattan distance adds absolute axis gaps instead. Match the distance to the measurement types and application; scaling cannot rescue irrelevant inputs.

Next: Choosing K ↓
06Choosing K

Why does K change the behavior?

Small K pays attention to a very local area, but one unusual neighbour can change the answer. Large K gives a broader vote, but may wash out smaller patterns or favour the common class.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
K = 1Nearest example decides
Larger KMore averaged evidence

Why this works · a little deeper

K is a hyperparameter: a setting chosen by validation rather than a fitted weight. Small K can follow noise; large K can miss local structure. Odd K avoids equal counts for uniform voting between two classes, but does not prevent every type of tie. There is no universally best K for every dataset.

08The Majority Vote

How do neighbors vote?

With uniform voting, each neighbour gets one equal vote. With distance weighting, closer neighbours count more. Exact matches and tied votes need a clear rule.

Why this works · a little deeper

A weighted vote can tie even with odd K. Multiclass voting can also tie. Inverse-distance weights cannot divide by zero. This notebook handles exact matches using the labels of those exact matches. A neighbour class fraction is a local estimate; it is not automatically a well-calibrated probability for future cases.

12The Effect of K

How do we choose K?

Try several K values using the same validation method. In cross-validation, each group takes a turn being held out. Fit input preparation inside each training turn. Prefer a useful, stable choice over a tiny uncertain lead.

Why this works · a little deeper

This lab computes leave-one-out cross-validation on its 48 training rows: hold out one training row, predict from the others, and repeat. Its scaling must also be fitted without the held-out row. In larger tasks, several-fold evaluation may be more practical; choose a split suited to time or related groups. A separate five-fold illustration has scores 75%,88%,92%,90% for K=1,3,5,7. An elbow is only a visual aid, not a replacement for scores and uncertainty. Refit the chosen procedure on all development training data before an untouched final evaluation.

13Strengths and Weaknesses

When does KNN become unreliable or expensive?

KNN struggles when distance stops being meaningful. Too many inputs, irrelevant clues, few nearby examples and very uneven class sizes can cause problems. Searching many stored points can also cost time and memory.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Why this works · a little deeper

Many inputs can make distances increasingly alike, called the curse of dimensionality. Common classes can dominate local votes. Brute-force prediction checks every stored point; search structures or approximations can reduce that cost but have their own limits. Validate meaningful coverage, rare-class errors, distance choices and resource use on the intended deployment population.
