01The Multi-Variable World

Why use more than one predictor?

One clue may not be enough to predict an outcome. Multiple regression combines several inputs, called predictors. Each row still has one outcome. The model adds up the contributions from all its predictors.

Why this works · a little deeper

With two inputs and one outcome, the prediction can be drawn as a plane in three dimensions. One dot is one observed row; its height is the outcome. The vertical gap to the plane is actual minus predicted. More inputs require more dimensions, so a drawing must clearly state which ones are held fixed. The live lab uses two inputs, with the same rows shown across its views.

Quick check · Does adding a predictor guarantee better new-data predictions?

No. It can add noise or overfitting.

02The Secret Recipe

How does the equation combine clues?

Start with the intercept, the prediction when all inputs are zero. Multiply each input by its coefficient, then add the products. The result is the model's predicted average outcome for those inputs.

Prediction58

Why this works · a little deeper

ŷ = b₀+b₁x₁+b₂x₂. ŷ means prediction, b₀ is the intercept, and b₁,b₂ are coefficients. Each coefficient has outcome units per input unit. The fitted plane describes the predicted mean; individual outcomes can differ. An intercept at impossible or unobserved zero inputs may have little practical meaning.

Quick check · What happens if only hours increase by one?

The prediction rises by 2, holding the other input fixed.

Why this works · a little deeper

Use the same observed rows while comparing trial coefficients. The residuals are vertical outcome gaps at each row's own input values. Quick check · Does a slice show every observation at its own second input?

No. Observations can sit away from a fixed-input slice.

04The Least Squares Method

How does least squares select the weights?

Least squares chooses all coefficients together to make squared prediction gaps as small as possible. To get one unique unpenalized coefficient answer, the input columns must not exactly repeat combinations of one another.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
ResidualObserved − predicted
ObjectiveSum of squared residuals
IdentifiabilityNo exact redundant predictor column

Your notebook: Open the visual to inspect the current calculation.

↳
Try it

Try a poor slope, then choose “Find the least-squares plane”. Check that the training error falls while the observations stay fixed.

Why this works · a little deeper

Squaring stops +4 and −4 cancelling: both contribute 16. Least squares minimizes their total across training rows. A design matrix is the table of model input columns, including the intercept. Exact column redundancy gives non-unique ordinary coefficients. A penalty can stabilize a fit, but changes the fitting rule and does not prove causal effects.

Quick check · Can two identical columns have uniquely determined separate coefficients?

No. Their combined effect can be identified, but their separate weights cannot.

05The Superpower: Holding Constant

What does holding constant mean?

Holding constant means keeping the other input numbers fixed inside the model. Change one input and see how the prediction changes. It does not literally freeze the world or prove a cause-and-effect relationship.

Keep fixedSame x₂ value
InterpretationAdjusted model association

Try it

Move x₁ along the green line while keeping x₂ fixed. The rise for a one-unit move is β₁. Then change the held value of x₂.

Why this works · a little deeper

If b₁=3, increasing x₁ by one raises the predicted mean by 3 outcome units while x₂ stays fixed. That is an adjusted model association, not a guaranteed gain for one person. Holding a variable fixed may be impossible as a real intervention. Causal interpretation needs a suitable design and assumptions beyond the regression equation.

Quick check · If two predictors nearly always move together, is this comparison well supported?

It may rely on sparse or nonexistent combinations.

07The Spurious Trap

Why can an apparent relationship disappear?

A relationship seen with one input alone can change when another input is included. The two inputs may share information. This adjusted relationship belongs to the fitted model; it does not automatically settle what causes what.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
UnadjustedOutcome related to x₁
AdjustedCompare x₁ at a fixed x₂
CautionAdjustment can also introduce bias

Try it

Compare the raw cloud with the residual cloud after removing x₂. These are the same observations; the adjusted slope can be very different.

Why this works · a little deeper

A shared cause can create an unadjusted link. Hot weather, for example, may influence both ice-cream sales and beach activity. Adding a relevant predictor can change another coefficient, but does not guarantee all confounding has been removed. A coefficient near zero means little adjusted linear association in this particular fit; it does not prove no real-world effect. Coefficient sizes also depend on units.

Quick check · Should every available variable be adjusted for in causal work?

No. Mediators, colliders, and post-outcome variables need careful treatment.

08The Adjusted R² Penalty

Why can training R-squared reward useless variables?

Adding inputs to an ordinary least-squares fit cannot lower training R² on the same rows. Even a useless input can get credit for chance patterns. Adjusted R² adds a penalty. Validation asks whether predictions improve on other rows.

Include and remove the seeded noise predictor. Compare training R² with adjusted R², then draw another sample to see a chance result change.

Why this works · a little deeper

For an ordinary least-squares model with an intercept, training R²=1−SSE/TSS. Adjusted R²=1−[(1−R²)(n−1)/(n−p−1)], with p predictors and a positive residual degrees-of-freedom denominator. Adjusted R² can rise or fall after adding a predictor. Neither score is an automatic feature-retention rule. Compare held-out prediction error and the purpose of the model too.

Quick check · Is the largest training R² a sufficient selection rule?

No. It rewards unnecessary flexibility.

09Reading the Model Output

How do I read a regression summary?

Read each coefficient with its units and uncertainty. Then check assumptions, prediction errors and validation results. Closely overlapping inputs can make individual coefficients uncertain even when combined predictions remain useful.

Increase outcome noise and watch the coefficient intervals widen. Read the dot as the estimate and the line as its model-based uncertainty.

Why this works · a little deeper

A coefficient's SE estimates its uncertainty under the model and design assumptions. t is estimate/SE for a zero-coefficient test; a universal cutoff of 2 is not always appropriate. Check residual shape, changing spread, dependence and influence. VIF measures overlap with other predictors. Distinguish an interval for a mean response from a wider interval for a new observation. State categorical reference levels, interactions, evaluation design and limits.

Quick check · Does a small coefficient p-value establish causation?

No. The study design and causal assumptions remain necessary.
