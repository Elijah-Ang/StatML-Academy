Why this works · a little deeper

A row is one observational unit; a feature is an input available at prediction time; a target is what we want to predict. Linear regression describes the conditional mean, E[y | x], as β₀ + β₁x. Real outcomes vary around that mean. Here all five rows are used for a transparent training example. For a predictive claim, keep independent observations for validation and a final test. A confounder, such as prior preparation, could influence both hours and scores.

Quick check · If two students study for three hours, must their scores match?

No. The line estimates a mean response. Individual scores can differ because of other influences and random variation.

02Make your guess

What do slope and intercept actually do?

Slope tilts the line: it is the predicted change in score for one extra hour. Intercept moves the whole line up or down: it is the prediction at zero hours.

Why this works · a little deeper

The hat in ŷ means an estimate, not the observed outcome y. A line is “linear” here because the unknown coefficients enter additively. The slope has outcome units divided by input units; the intercept has outcome units. An intercept may lie outside the observed x range, so its numerical meaning need not have a useful real-world interpretation. A negative slope is allowed. The displayed trial line is your choice; it is not yet the least-squares fit.

Quick check · With slope 7, what does two more hours change in the prediction?

The prediction rises by 14 score points. This is a model-based association, not a guaranteed individual improvement.

Why this works · a little deeper

Residuals are observed training errors, eᵢ = yᵢ − ŷᵢ. They estimate the unobserved disturbances in the population model. Ordinary least squares measures vertical discrepancies, not the shortest perpendicular distance to the line. Perpendicular distances answer a different problem in which variation in both coordinates is treated symmetrically. A residual of −4 means the prediction was 4 points too high. A residual of +4 means it was 4 points too low.

Quick check · The actual score is 61 and the predicted score is 65. What is the residual?

61 − 65 = −4. The model overpredicted by four points.

04A penalty that cannot cancel

Why square the errors?

Adding signed residuals can hide mistakes: +4 and −4 sum to zero. Squaring keeps both penalties positive and gives large misses extra weight.

Why this works · a little deeper

SSE means sum of squared errors: Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)². Squaring creates a smooth objective with a closed-form solution for a full-rank linear model. It also makes least squares sensitive to unusual large residuals. MAE, the mean absolute error, penalizes misses proportionally and can be more robust; minimizing it is a different fitting problem. The visual tiles use an area proportional to squared residual. They are an error-size cue, not a new x-coordinate measurement. SSE has squared outcome units; RMSE and RSE return to outcome units.

Quick check · Which contributes more to SSE: one residual of 8 or two residuals of 4?

The residual of 8 contributes 64. Two residuals of 4 contribute 16 + 16 = 32.

Next: The least-squares engine ↓
05The least-squares engine

How does the best line get its numbers?

First find the average x and average y. Then measure how they vary together. Divide that shared movement by the spread in x to get the slope; choose the intercept so the line passes through the two averages.

CalculationLive result
Average x, average y3.0, 60.0
Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ)49.0
Σ(x − x̄)²10.0
m = shared movement / x spread4.90

Why this works · a little deeper

Differentiating SSE with respect to b and m and setting both derivatives to zero gives the normal equations. Solving them yields the formula above and b = ȳ − mx̄. When x varies, this is the unique minimum for an intercept-and-slope fit. If every x is identical, the denominator is zero: slope and intercept cannot be identified separately. You can still estimate the mean at that observed x, but there is no unique regression slope. No iterative gradient descent is needed for this tiny model.

Quick check · Why can’t we learn a slope if every student has exactly three study hours?

There is no variation in x to compare. Many slope/intercept pairs give the same prediction at x = 3.

What does “best” promise?

It promises the smallest training SSE among all straight lines with an intercept. It does not promise the right causal story, reliable extrapolation, or the smallest error on future students.

Why this works · a little deeper

With an intercept, the fitted residuals sum to zero (up to numerical precision), and the fitted line passes through (x̄, ȳ). These are algebraic properties of least squares, not checks that the scientific assumptions hold. A very small training error can coexist with a biased dataset. Compare predictive performance on held-out data and inspect residual patterns. If the relationship curves, a straight-line family may simply be too restrictive.

Quick check · Can another straight line have a smaller training SSE than this fit?

Not when the same rows, squared-error objective and intercept are used and x varies. Another model family or objective is a different comparison.

Why this works · a little deeper

Two fitted coefficients consume two degrees of freedom, hence n − 2 in RSE. With an intercept, training R² lies between 0 and 1 when y varies. Held-out R² can be negative if the model is worse than the comparison baseline; when all y values are identical, this R² definition divides by zero and is undefined. Inspect residual-versus-fitted plots for curvature and changing spread; use leverage and Cook’s distance for influence. Independence comes from study design. Classical small-sample inference needs additional error assumptions. A confidence interval covers uncertainty in the estimated mean response; a prediction interval for a new individual is wider because it also includes individual variation. No interval is drawn here.

Quick check · Does an R² of 0.99 prove that study hours cause exam performance?

No. It describes a strong fit on these observations. Confounding, selection and future prediction remain separate questions.

08Predict with care

When is the line being asked to guess too far?

Interpolation stays within the observed x range. Extrapolation goes outside it, where the pattern may change. The line can return a number anywhere; that does not make every number credible.

Why this works · a little deeper

Use this workflow: define the prediction time and outcome; split independent units; fit on training data; inspect residuals and influential observations; compare against a relevant baseline on validation data; lock the method; evaluate on the final test. Good prediction also requires the new population to resemble the data used for development. A coefficient is an adjusted association only relative to the variables in the model; causal claims need a defensible causal design. For this demonstration, the fitted line and all scorecard values use the five displayed rows, so they are training quantities.

Quick check · Is a prediction at x = 4 or x = 20 better supported by these original observations?

x = 4. The original observed range is 1–5. x = 20 is a distant extrapolation.
