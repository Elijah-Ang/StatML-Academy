01The Raw Data

What makes observations paired?

Each dot must join two measurements from the same person or object. One student's hours and that same student's score make one pair. Keep the pair together when you sort or clean the data.

03Measuring Strength: r

What does r measure?

Pearson's r describes how closely the dots follow a straight-line pattern. It runs from −1 to +1. Positive means they tend to rise together; negative means one tends to fall as the other rises. It does not measure cause or prediction error.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
r = +1All points on an increasing line
r = 0No linear association in the sample
r = −1All points on a decreasing line

Why this works · a little deeper

r = +1 means all varying pairs fall on an increasing straight line. r = −1 means they fall on a decreasing straight line. r = 0 means no linear association in these pairs. It does not exclude curved relationships. The calculation divides covariance, a measure of shared movement, by the two standard deviations. Both measurements need nonzero spread for r to be defined.

Quick check · Does converting metres to centimetres change r?

No, provided the conversion is a positive linear rescaling.

Next: Formal Hypotheses ↓
04Formal Hypotheses

What is the null about?

The usual test starts with a model where the population's straight-line correlation is zero. Its p-value asks how unusual the result would be under that model and its assumptions. It is not the chance that the zero-correlation model is true.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Nullρ = 0
Sample statisticr
Common testt = r√((n − 2)/(1 − r²))

Why this works · a little deeper

ρ, pronounced rho, names population correlation. r names the sample estimate. H₀: ρ = 0 is the null model; H₁: ρ ≠ 0 is the alternative. The alternative says correlation is nonzero, not automatically statistically significant. The usual t statistic is r√((n − 2)/(1 − r²)); its test needs independent pairs and suitable distributional assumptions. Failure to reject does not prove independence.

Quick check · Does failing to reject prove independence?

No. Nonlinear dependence may remain, and the study may be imprecise.

05How it works: Covariability

Why do matching deviation signs increase correlation?

Subtract each measurement's average. Multiply the two gaps for each pair. Matching signs give a positive product; opposite signs give a negative one. Combine and scale these products to calculate r.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Above both meansPositive product
Below both meansPositive product
Above x, below yNegative product

Why this works · a little deeper

The symbol Σ means add across all pairs. A bar over a letter means its average. A positive gap times a positive gap is positive; two negative gaps also give a positive product. For gaps +2 and −3, the product is −6. The bottom of the r formula scales the total using each measurement's spread, making r free of units.

Quick check · What is the product for deviations +2 and −3?

−6. This pair pulls the covariance downward.

06Assumptions: Paired & Linear

Can r miss a strong pattern?

One number cannot show every shape. A strong U-shaped pattern can have r near zero because the upward and downward parts cancel. A near-zero r does not mean there is no relationship.

Why this works · a little deeper

Pearson r can be calculated for numeric pairs even when the pattern is curved; the problem is interpreting that number as the whole relationship. Spearman describes a pattern that keeps rising or keeps falling, even if it curves. Such a pattern is called monotonic. It can also miss a U-shape because a U-shape changes direction.

Quick check · Would Spearman correlation fully describe a symmetric U-shape?

No. Spearman also targets a relationship that keeps rising or keeps falling.

Why this works · a little deeper

A p-value is calculated assuming the null model and test assumptions. It concerns a result at least this extreme, not the probability that chance caused the result. Report the sample size, r, an appropriate interval, shape and data collection method. Practical importance depends on the application, not only on crossing p = 0.05.

Quick check · Can a weak correlation be statistically detectable in a large sample?

Yes. Detectability does not establish practical importance.

Next: The Golden Rule: Causation ↓
09The Golden Rule: Causation

Why does association not establish cause?

Two things can move together because one affects the other, because both share another cause, or because of how the data was selected. Correlation alone cannot tell these explanations apart.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Shared causeHot weather raises ice-cream sales and swimming
Observed associationSales and drownings can rise together
Causal conclusionNeeds a credible design and assumptions

Why this works · a little deeper

A confounder is another cause linked to both quantities. Hot weather can raise ice-cream sales and swimming, creating an association without ice cream causing drowning. Selection can also create links. Adjusting for one measured factor does not guarantee all hidden causes have been handled. Cause-and-effect conclusions require a suitable design and assumptions.

Quick check · Does controlling one variable automatically remove all confounding?

No. Important unmeasured or misspecified causes may remain.

10Limitations & Summary

Which correlation should I use?

Choose the measure for the pattern. Pearson uses the numerical values to describe a straight-line relationship. Spearman and Kendall use order or ranks. A rank is a value's position when the values are sorted.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Why this works · a little deeper

Changing metres to centimetres keeps r the same because it is a positive linear change of units. Pearson summarizes numeric linear association. Spearman compares ranks, while Kendall compares agreement in pair ordering. Neither rank measure automatically fixes unusual data, related observations, hidden causes or patterns that change direction. Plot the pairs and explain why the chosen measure suits the question.

Quick check · Which measure suits ordered categories and a pattern that keeps rising?

A rank-based measure such as Spearman or Kendall may suit that question. Check the plot and measurement meaning too.
