Why this works · a little deeper

Unsupervised does not mean assumption-free. The feature choice, units, distance and K encode what similarity means. A discovered cluster is an algorithmic grouping, not automatically a natural type of person. These 36 synthetic observations are a teaching dataset. Both raw-unit and z-score distance are available; the axes continue to show original units so you can see that changing the metric does not change the customers.

Quick check · Does a blue cluster imply a known customer category?

No. Color identifies a proposed group. Interpretation requires domain knowledge and external evidence.

Why does the starting point matter?

K-means starts with K candidate centers. Our reproducible initializer chooses K distinct observations as centers. Different starts can lead to different final groupings.

Method: random observations (Forgy).

Why this works · a little deeper

We use a seeded shuffle so a seed produces the same distinct starting observations. K-means++ instead spreads seeds with a probability related to squared distance; it often improves initialization but does not guarantee the global optimum. Random partition initialization is yet another method. Do not confuse these methods. Multiple restarts compare complete fits under the same distance and dataset. K is an input, not something Lloyd’s iterations discover automatically.

Quick check · Does changing the seed create different customers?

No. It only changes initial centers. The points remain fixed.

Why this works · a little deeper

Euclidean distance is √Σⱼ(xⱼ − cⱼ)². Minimizing it is equivalent to minimizing its square, so the assignment calculation can omit the square root. The cluster colors follow these exact calculations. Equal distances are resolved consistently in favor of the lower center number. The display uses original units, but standardized mode measures distances in z-score coordinates. Visual closeness on a resized plot must not replace the numerical distance calculation.

Quick check · Do we move a center immediately after assigning one point?

No. Assign every point using the same current centers, then update the centers as a separate phase.

Next: Recenter by averaging ↓
04Recenter by averaging

Why does the center move to the mean?

For each group, average its members’ coordinates. That average is the location that minimizes their total squared distance while their memberships stay fixed.

new center = (mean x, mean y)

Phase: Initialized
Completed recentering rounds: 0

↳
Try it

After assignment, press Next step again. Watch the centers move while memberships stay fixed.

Why this works · a little deeper

Each coordinate minimizes a sum of squared deviations at its arithmetic mean. This is why the method is called K-means, and why squared Euclidean distance and the mean belong together. Replacing the distance with Manhattan distance would call for coordinate medians, a different objective. If a cluster has no members, its mean is undefined. This demonstration retains its previous center and reports the empty cluster; other implementations can reseed it. During a visual transition the committed calculation is shown, and intermediate positions are only a movement cue.

Quick check · Must the new center coincide with an observed customer?

No. The average may lie between observations.

Why this works · a little deeper

Under the squared Euclidean objective, assignment and recentering cannot increase WCSS. That descent leads to a local solution; it does not guarantee the globally smallest WCSS. Scrolling to a different lesson stage does not rerun or reset the algorithm. Changing K, scaling, scenario or the initialization seed explicitly starts a new experiment. Compare solutions across starts and assess stability. Iteration counts alone are not evidence that the groups are useful.

Quick check · Can two runs converge to different answers on exactly the same data?

Yes. Different initial centers can lead to different local minima.

Next: Measure within-cluster spread ↓
06Measure within-cluster spread

What is WCSS actually adding up?

WCSS adds every point’s squared distance to its assigned center. It measures compactness in the chosen feature space. Smaller is better for this objective, with important limits.

WCSS = Σᵢ ‖xᵢ − c(assigned i)‖²

Why this works · a little deeper

WCSS is also called inertia. It depends on feature units, scaling, the number of observations and K. A value from raw-dollar distance is not directly comparable with a z-score WCSS. For a fixed representation, the optimal objective cannot rise as K increases, because additional centers can reproduce an earlier solution. A finite multi-start heuristic need not find those optima. A low value alone does not establish meaningful, stable or fair segments.

Quick check · Can we compare WCSS = 20 after standardization with WCSS = 500 in raw units?

Not as evidence that one fit is better: the distance units and therefore the objective have changed.

Next: Look for an elbow ↓
07Look for an elbow

How many groups earn their complexity?

Fit several values of K and inspect how much each extra center reduces WCSS. A bend is a clue that additional groups bring smaller gains. It is a judgment aid, not an automatic answer.

A real computation on these points

The curve fits K = 1…6 with five fixed starts per K and retains the smallest WCSS found. Select a point on the curve to try that K.

↳
Try it

Switch the visual to the elbow curve. Choose a K near the bend and inspect its groups.

Why this works · a little deeper

The plot recomputes from the displayed scenario and chosen feature scaling; it is not a decorative pre-drawn curve. Multiple starts reduce initialization sensitivity. Sometimes no clear elbow exists, and real structure may be gradual or hierarchical. Use stability across resamples, silhouette analysis, external evidence and the intended decision alongside compactness. A silhouette compares within-cluster closeness with separation from other clusters; it still inherits the metric’s assumptions. More segments also carry a practical interpretation and maintenance cost.

Quick check · If the curve bends smoothly with no obvious corner, is K-means broken?

No. There may be no uniquely compelling K. State the ambiguity and use additional evidence.

Next: Choose what distance means ↓
08Choose what distance means

Should a dollar count as much as a year?

In raw units, spending differences can dominate age differences because their numbers are larger. Standardization expresses each feature in its own standard-deviation units. That changes the question of similarity.

Try it

Choose raw units or standardized units, then press Run. The same customers stay in place while the fitted groups change.

Why this works · a little deeper

A z-score is (x − training mean) / training standard deviation. Standardization is appropriate when relative variation across features should have comparable influence; it is not a universal rule. Domain weights may be preferable, and scaling cannot rescue meaningless features. If clustering is part of a predictive pipeline, fit its scaler and centers inside each training fold. For exploratory analysis here, all displayed observations define the scaler. Standardization is sensitive to outliers; robust scaling is another choice to justify.

Quick check · Does standardizing prove that age and spending are equally important?

No. It imposes a particular distance convention. Importance is a domain question, not a consequence of unit conversion.

Next: Find the limits ↓
09Find the limits

When do neat circles tell the wrong story?

K-means favors compact groups around means. Curved shapes, very different densities and outliers can make those groups misleading. An algorithm can converge cleanly to an unhelpful description.

Try two stress tests: curved moons and one unusually large spender.

What to report: features, scaling, K, initialization, restarts, final WCSS, stability and why the segments are useful.

↳
Try it

Choose Curved moons or Add outlier, then run. Describe what the method missed.

Why this works · a little deeper

Means move toward extreme observations, which can consume a center or distort other groups. Non-convex shapes may be split across several compact regions even when a human sees one curved group. DBSCAN can represent density-connected shapes but needs meaningful density settings; hierarchical clustering answers a different family of questions. Neither is universally better. Consider the data geometry and deployment needs. A new observation can be assigned to a fitted center using the same trained feature transformation; refitting everything changes the segments.

Quick check · Does reaching “Converged” establish that the clusters are real?

No. It only says this optimization procedure stopped changing assignments. Geometry, stability and domain meaning still need evaluation.
