01The Unsupervised Explorer

What can we learn without a target?

PCA finds directions in which the input data varies. It does not use an answer label. It can reveal a pattern or help store fewer coordinates, but it does not tell us what caused that pattern.

Why this works · a little deeper

The live example has eight observations and two features. Centre each column by subtracting its average. Sample covariance is XᵀX/(n−1) for the centred table X. Eigenvectors are the component directions; eigenvalues are their score variances. These names describe the calculation, not a guarantee that the directions are scientifically meaningful.

Quick check · Is PCA supervised when labels are shown only for coloring?

No. Labels do not enter the fit in that case.

02The "Too Much Information" Problem

Why compress several measurements?

Compression replaces several coordinates with fewer new ones. This makes the representation smaller. It also loses some variation, which might still matter for a later task.

Why this works · a little deeper

With six inputs there are 15 pairwise plots; with ten there are 45. PCA gives new combined coordinates rather than pretending one old axis shows everything. Correlated inputs can share information, but small-variance directions may still matter. Compression should be chosen for a stated purpose.

Quick check · Does low variance always mean useless information?

No. A low-variance direction may matter for a target.

03Finding the First Principal Component

Why maximize projected variance?

Project the centred points onto a line. PC1 is the direction where those projected points spread out most. For one linear component, it also gives the smallest total squared reconstruction gaps.

Why this works · a little deeper

Projection assigns each centred point a position on the candidate unit-length line. PCA maximizes the variance of those positions. Equivalently, it minimizes squared perpendicular reconstruction distance to that line. This equivalence concerns the chosen centred numeric scale and squared error, not all kinds of information or distance.

Quick check · Why require a unit-length direction?

Otherwise multiplying the direction could increase the variance arbitrarily.

Next: The Secret Sauce: Loadings ↓
04The Secret Sauce: Loadings

What are loadings?

Loadings are the weights that define a new direction. They tell us how the original inputs combine. Flipping all their signs reverses the axis but leaves the same line and represented subspace.

Why this works · a little deeper

A component score is the sum of loading×centred input contributions. If scaling is selected, use the scaled inputs instead. Directions have unit length and are perpendicular. Reversing every loading and score sign produces the same reconstruction, so signs alone do not establish a uniquely positive or negative scientific factor.

Quick check · Is a sign-reversed PC1 a different solution?

No. It describes the same axis.

05When Scaling Is Appropriate

Why might scaling change the answer?

PCA uses input spread, so units matter. Standardizing puts spread relative to each feature's own scale. That changes the question PCA answers. Decide whether raw-unit spread or relative spread is meaningful here.

Why this works · a little deeper

Standardization subtracts the training mean and divides by the training SD. It puts variables on a comparable relative-spread scale. If units differ, this may be useful; if common-unit variance differences carry meaning, it may be unwanted. Zero-spread columns need explicit handling. Fit any scaler and PCA only on training data for a predictive evaluation.

Quick check · Is standardization mandatory for every PCA?

No.

06Capturing the Leftovers: PC2

How is PC2 selected?

PC2 captures the largest remaining projected spread while staying perpendicular to PC1. In two inputs, the two directions form a rotated coordinate system at right angles.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Try it

Keep standardization off and change z measurement units. Watch both fitted axes change while staying at right angles. Then standardize and compare the effect of changing units.

Why this works · a little deeper

Perpendicular means at 90 degrees. In the fitted sample covariance, component scores are uncorrelated. This does not generally mean independent or causally unrelated. Later population data need not have exactly the same fitted covariance. A second direction is chosen for remaining variance, not for an outcome label.

Quick check · Are orthogonal PCA scores necessarily statistically independent?

No. They are uncorrelated in the fitted sample, not generally independent.

Next: The Transformation (Scores) ↓
07The Transformation (Scores)

What are scores?

A score is one row's position along a component. A loading belongs to the direction; a score belongs to the row. Do not confuse these two kinds of numbers.

Why this works · a little deeper

Keeping all components can reconstruct centred inputs up to numerical precision. Keeping fewer leaves reconstruction gaps. Scores are new row coordinates, not model probabilities. Loadings are direction weights, not rows. Inspect original and reconstructed coordinates for the same selected observation.

Quick check · Do two observations share the same score simply because loadings are shared?

No. Different input values generally give different scores.

Next: Proportion of Variance Explained ↓
08Proportion of Variance Explained

What fraction of variance is retained?

Explained-variance ratio is a component's variance divided by total variance in the chosen scale. It tells us how much squared spread remains. It does not guarantee that the same fraction of meaning or predictive information remains.

Why this works · a little deeper

The scree bars show component variances or their ratios; the cumulative line adds retained sample variance. An elbow can help describe the pattern, but does not prove a best component count. A low-variance component can still predict an important target. Discarding components only reduces noise when those directions actually contain mostly noise.

Quick check · Does 80% explained variance imply 80% classification accuracy?

No.

What should I report and avoid claiming?
