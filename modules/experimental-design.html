Why this works · a little deeper

The estimand is the specific quantity we want to learn. For example, average score difference after one term between two assigned teaching strategies in eligible schools. A sophisticated test cannot repair a design that never collected the needed comparison. Distinguish the chosen population and strategy from a vague claim that something works.

Quick check · Does a p-value specify the effect we want to estimate?

No. The estimand specifies the target comparison. A p-value is one test result about a model.

02Intuition and objective

What does random assignment accomplish?

Random assignment chooses treatment by chance. Across repeated assignments it balances other factors on average, but one actual study may still be unbalanced. Random sampling instead concerns who enters the study.

Why this works · a little deeper

A confounder is a factor associated with both treatment and outcome. Random assignment avoids deliberately choosing treatment based on those factors and balances them in expectation. It does not guarantee exact balance in this sample. Random sampling concerns population representation, so a randomized treatment study can still use a nonrepresentative volunteer sample.

Quick check · Does random assignment guarantee identical baseline means?

No.

Worked example

Why are twenty schools not six hundred independent assignments?

If twenty schools are assigned, the school is the assignment unit. Students in one school share a treatment and may have related outcomes. Six hundred student rows do not mean six hundred independent assignments.

Twenty schools assigned with 30 students each produce 600 measurements, but only 20 assignment units. Outcomes within schools may be correlated. Analyze and plan precision for that group structure rather than pretending each student received an independent random assignment. The needed method depends on the design and target.

04Reasoning with the method

How do blocking, blinding, and intention-to-treat differ?

Blocking groups similar participants before random assignment. Blinding hides assignment from people whose behavior or measurements might change. Intention-to-treat compares the groups as assigned. These methods solve different problems.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

BlindingHide assignment where appropriate
Intention-to-treatRetain assigned groups

Why this works · a little deeper

Allocation concealment prevents knowing the next assignment during enrollment. Blinding reduces changes in behavior or measurement from knowing assignment. Blocking randomizes within relevant similar groups. Intention-to-treat compares assigned strategies, including imperfect adherence; per-protocol comparison concerns those who followed the protocol and can be more sensitive to selection. Plan missingness, spillover and adherence before results are known.

Quick check · Does intention-to-treat mean discarding noncompliant participants?

No.

05Interactive laboratory

How does clustering affect effective information?

Related people in one group may carry overlapping information. For equal group sizes, a simple design-effect formula estimates how much variance is increased. Its effective sample size is a rough comparison, not a complete power calculation.

Why this works · a little deeper

For group size m and within-group correlation ICC, design effect ≈1+(m−1)ICC. For m=30 and ICC=.05, it is 2.45; 600/2.45≈245 is a rough information comparison. The formula has assumptions and does not replace power planning. Number of groups, unequal sizes, allocation, variance, effect, missingness and analysis method also matter. The lab illustrates this formula only.

Quick check · Is effective n a substitute for a full cluster-trial power calculation?

No.

06Assumptions and failure modes

What can break a randomized comparison?

People may drop out, not follow the assigned treatment, or affect other groups. Outcomes or measurement may also change. Plan for these possibilities and explain how they affect the question we can answer.

Follow the ideaRead it in plain language

Outcome switchingChanges the planned question

Try it

Inspect the crossed pupils in the follow-up illustration. Explain how losing different people can select the observed sample; these are illustrative missing outcomes, not measured rates.

Why this works · a little deeper

Assumptions

Assignment, measurement and follow-up must support the intended comparison. Account for related groups, interference, nonadherence and missing outcomes where relevant.

When the method fails

Attrition means missing follow-up outcomes. Noncompliance means not following assignment. Interference or spillover means one unit's treatment affects another's outcome. Differential losses or changing the outcome after seeing results can undermine the intended comparison. State what was observed and the assumptions needed for the resulting effect estimate.

Quick check · Does randomization automatically repair differential follow-up loss?

No.
