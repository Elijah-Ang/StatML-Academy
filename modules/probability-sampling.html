02Intuition and objective

How are probability, density, and frequency different?

Discrete probabilities add over outcomes. A continuous density gives probability through area over an interval, not through the curve's height at one point. Frequency is what actually occurred in a sample.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

Conditional probability P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B), for P(B) > 0, means the chance of A given B. Independence means P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B): knowing one event occurred does not change the chance of the other. Expectation E[X] is the probability-weighted average of a random variable; variance Var(X) = E[(X − E[X])²] measures its spread, when these quantities exist. A population distribution describes individual values. A sampling distribution describes a statistic—such as a mean—across repeated samples. Its standard deviation is the standard error.

How is a standard error calculated?

Standard deviation describes individuals. Standard error describes an estimator across repeated samples. For an independent sample mean, estimated SE is the sample SD divided by √n.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

Discrete probability masses add directly, whereas continuous density integrates to probability over an interval. Density height is not itself point probability and may exceed one. A statistic is a random variable before data are observed, and its sampling distribution depends on the population, statistic and design. The central limit theorem supports many normal approximations but does not make every small or heavy-tailed sample normal. Standard deviation describes individual variation; standard error describes estimator variation. Clustering, unequal weights and finite-population sampling change the familiar independent-sample formula and the effective amount of information.

06Assumptions and failure modes

When is the usual SE misleading?

The familiar SE formula assumes independent information. Clustered observations can share errors, and heavy tails can make small-sample approximations unreliable. More rows need not mean proportionally more information.

Assumptions

Repeated-sampling claims presume the same population and design. The usual √n improvement requires independent or appropriately modelled observations.

When the method fails

Density height is not point probability; dependence reduces effective sample size; skew and heavy tails can slow normal approximations; convenience samples do not become representative by growing.

09Knowledge checks

Can I tell SD from SE?

Ask what is varying. If you describe how people differ, use SD. If you describe how a mean would vary across repeated samples, use SE.

A small example · separate from the live dataset
Follow the ideaRead it in plain language
Individual spreadSD
Mean-estimate spreadSE
Example: SD 15, n 25SE = 3
A sampling distribution describes…

Answer and explanation

A statistic across repeated samples. Its random object is the statistic produced by the sampling procedure.

Why does standard error often shrink with n?

Answer and explanation

Independent observations add information and averaging reduces noise. For a mean under independence, SE scales approximately as 1/√n.

Is a continuous density height a probability?

Answer and explanation

No; probability is area over an interval. A point has zero probability in a continuous model; interval area is probability.

If observations are clustered, naive SE is often…

Answer and explanation

Too small. Within-cluster similarity means less independent information than the row count suggests.

To halve a mean’s SE, n must be approximately…

Answer and explanation

Four times as large. Because SE is proportional to 1/√n, halving it requires quadrupling n.
