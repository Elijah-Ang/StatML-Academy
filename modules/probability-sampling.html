Stage 01

The problem

Observed statistics vary from sample to sample. Probability provides a language for that uncertainty and sampling distributions describe how an estimator behaves under repeated study designs.

Stage 02

Intuition and objective

A population distribution describes individual values. A sampling distribution describes a statistic—such as a mean—across repeated samples. Its standard deviation is the standard error.

Stage 03

Worked example

If individual scores have standard deviation 12, the mean of n=36 independent observations has standard error about 12/√36=2. Doubling n does not halve uncertainty; roughly quadrupling n does.

Stage 04

Reasoning with the method

Discrete probability masses add directly, whereas continuous density integrates to probability over an interval. Density height is not itself point probability and may exceed one. A statistic is a random variable before data are observed, and its sampling distribution depends on the population, statistic and design. The central limit theorem supports many normal approximations but does not make every small or heavy-tailed sample normal. Standard deviation describes individual variation; standard error describes estimator variation. Clustering, unequal weights and finite-population sampling change the familiar independent-sample formula and the effective amount of information.

A defensible analysis connects the mathematical target to the real decision. Identify what is estimated, which observations contribute independent information, what choices are learned from data, and which uncertainty remains after fitting. Then ask how the conclusion would change under another reasonable sample, a plausible alternative specification, or a shift in the deployment population. This reasoning must happen before the final test set is opened. The test result evaluates the locked procedure; it does not choose that procedure. Sensitivity analysis is most useful when it is planned around credible failure modes rather than used to search for a preferred answer.

Stage 05

Interactive laboratory

Choose sample size and draw repeated samples. The histogram of sample means narrows as independent information increases.

Stage 06

Assumptions and failure modes

Assumptions

Repeated-sampling claims presume the same population and design. The usual √n improvement requires independent or appropriately modelled observations.

When the method fails

Density height is not point probability; dependence reduces effective sample size; skew and heavy tails can slow normal approximations; convenience samples do not become representative by growing.

Stage 07

Practical workflow

  1. Define population and random variable
  2. Identify discrete or continuous support
  3. Specify the sampling design
  4. Choose a statistic
  5. Study or approximate its sampling distribution
  6. Quantify standard error

Stage 08

Interpretation and reporting

What to report: Name the population, sample mechanism, statistic, sample size, dependence structure, estimated standard error and approximation used.

Common misuse: Confusing the spread of individual observations with the uncertainty of their mean.

Stage 09

Knowledge checks

A sampling distribution describes…

Answer and explanation

A statistic across repeated samples. Its random object is the statistic produced by the sampling procedure.

Why does standard error often shrink with n?

Answer and explanation

Independent observations add information and averaging reduces noise. For a mean under independence, SE scales approximately as 1/√n.

Is a continuous density height a probability?

Answer and explanation

No; probability is area over an interval. A point has zero probability in a continuous model; interval area is probability.

If observations are clustered, naive SE is often…

Answer and explanation

Too small. Within-cluster similarity means less independent information than the row count suggests.

To halve a mean’s SE, n must be approximately…

Answer and explanation

Four times as large. Because SE is proportional to 1/√n, halving it requires quadrupling n.
