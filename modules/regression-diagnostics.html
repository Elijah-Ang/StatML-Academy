Stage 01

The problem

A fitted equation always returns numbers; diagnostics ask whether the functional form, error behaviour, and individual observations make those numbers defensible.

Stage 02

Intuition and objective

Residuals should fluctuate without systematic shape around zero. Curvature signals missing functional form, a funnel signals changing variance, a Q–Q departure challenges normal-error inference, and an isolated high-leverage point can rotate the fitted line.

Stage 03

Worked example

A salary model has residual spread of ±5k for junior staff and ±40k for senior staff. The fan pattern violates constant variance. Coefficients may still describe a mean pattern, but ordinary standard errors and a single-width prediction band are unreliable; robust errors or a variance model may be needed.

Stage 04

Reasoning with the method

Residual-versus-fitted plots examine mean form and variance structure. Q–Q plots compare ordered residuals with a reference distribution and matter for inference that relies on that reference; predictors and outcomes need not themselves be normal. Leverage describes unusual predictor combinations. Influence describes how fitted results change, so Cook’s distance is a screening tool rather than a deletion command. VIF shows how strongly a predictor overlaps the others and can explain wide coefficient intervals without proving bias. Diagnostics should prompt a scientific response: correct errors, represent nonlinearity, model variance or dependence, and report justified sensitivity analyses.

A defensible analysis connects the mathematical target to the real decision. Identify what is estimated, which observations contribute independent information, what choices are learned from data, and which uncertainty remains after fitting. Then ask how the conclusion would change under another reasonable sample, a plausible alternative specification, or a shift in the deployment population. This reasoning must happen before the final test set is opened. The test result evaluates the locked procedure; it does not choose that procedure. Sensitivity analysis is most useful when it is planned around credible failure modes rather than used to search for a preferred answer.

Stage 05

Interactive laboratory

Switch between healthy, curved, funnel-shaped, and influential residual patterns. The plot and recommended response update together.

Stage 06

Assumptions and failure modes

Assumptions

Correct functional form, independent errors, stable conditional variance, and residual normality when classical small-sample inference needs it.

When the method fails

Outliers, high leverage, extrapolation, omitted nonlinearities, dependence and multicollinearity can destabilise estimates or uncertainty.

Stage 07

Practical workflow

  1. Plot outcome and predictors
  2. Fit the pre-specified model
  3. Inspect residual–fitted and Q–Q plots
  4. Check leverage, Cook’s distance and VIF
  5. Run justified sensitivity analyses
  6. Validate predictions on unseen data

Stage 08

Interpretation and reporting

What to report: Report coefficients with confidence intervals, residual standard error, diagnostic findings, influential-case sensitivity, validation error, and prediction intervals.

Common misuse: Deleting observations solely because their removal produces significance.

Stage 09

Knowledge checks

A widening residual funnel indicates…

Answer and explanation

Heteroscedasticity. Residual variance changes with the fitted value.

Leverage differs from influence because…

Answer and explanation

Leverage describes unusual predictor position; influence describes effect on the fit. Influence combines position and discrepancy to measure how much the fit changes.

For one future observation use…

Answer and explanation

A prediction interval. Prediction intervals include mean-estimation uncertainty and individual noise.

A curved residual pattern suggests…

Answer and explanation

Missing functional form. The mean relationship has not been represented adequately.

A high R² proves…

Answer and explanation

None of correctness, causality, or transportability. R² alone cannot validate the model.
