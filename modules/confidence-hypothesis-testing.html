Stage 01

The problem

Inference connects a noisy sample to a population quantity. Confidence intervals show compatible effect sizes; tests quantify how surprising the data are under a specified null model.

Stage 02

Intuition and objective

A p-value is computed assuming the null and asks about results at least as extreme. A confidence procedure is calibrated across repeated samples. Neither replaces effect size, assumptions, or practical meaning.

Stage 03

Worked example

An estimated mean difference is 4 points with a 95% CI from 1 to 7 and p=.01. The data are inconsistent with zero under the test assumptions, while plausible effects range from small to substantial. The p-value is not a 1% probability that the null is true.

Stage 04

Reasoning with the method

A p-value is a tail probability calculated under H₀ and every model assumption; the null is conditioned on, not assigned a probability. A compatible confidence interval often inverts a family of tests. Sample size affects precision, so tiny effects can be statistically clear and important effects can remain uncertain. Power is planned against a specific meaningful alternative and is not the probability that an observed finding is true. Family-wise and false-discovery-rate procedures answer different multiplicity goals. Neither repairs optional stopping, outcome switching or selective reporting after results are known.

A defensible analysis connects the mathematical target to the real decision. Identify what is estimated, which observations contribute independent information, what choices are learned from data, and which uncertainty remains after fitting. Then ask how the conclusion would change under another reasonable sample, a plausible alternative specification, or a shift in the deployment population. This reasoning must happen before the final test set is opened. The test result evaluates the locked procedure; it does not choose that procedure. Sensitivity analysis is most useful when it is planned around credible failure modes rather than used to search for a preferred answer.

Stage 05

Interactive laboratory

Change sample size and effect size. See the standard error, confidence interval and approximate p-value change—without changing what a p-value means.

Stage 06

Assumptions and failure modes

Assumptions

The test, estimator, sampling design and dependence model must match. Pre-specified hypotheses protect the advertised error rate.

When the method fails

Optional stopping, many uncorrected tests, model misspecification, low power and selective reporting invalidate simple interpretations.

Stage 07

Practical workflow

  1. Define estimand, H₀ and H₁
  2. Choose α and analysis before results
  3. Check design and assumptions
  4. Compute estimate, effect size, interval and p-value
  5. Adjust for multiplicity when required
  6. Interpret practical significance

Stage 08

Interpretation and reporting

What to report: Report estimate and compatible interval, exact p-value where useful, effect size, assumptions, multiplicity plan, sample size and practical consequences.

Common misuse: Calling p>.05 “no effect” or p<.05 “proof.”

Stage 09

Knowledge checks

A 95% confidence procedure means…

Answer and explanation

Across repetitions, 95% of intervals capture the parameter under assumptions. Frequentist coverage is a long-run property of the procedure.

A p-value is not…

Answer and explanation

The probability the null is true. The null is conditioned on when calculating the p-value.

Testing 20 outcomes requires attention to…

Answer and explanation

Multiplicity. Multiple opportunities for small p-values inflate false positives.

Failing to reject means…

Answer and explanation

Evidence was insufficient at the chosen rule. Absence of sufficient evidence is not evidence of exact absence.

What completes an inferential result?

Answer and explanation

Effect size, interval and practical interpretation. Magnitude and uncertainty are required for useful interpretation.
