Instead of guessing, the computer uses matrix algebra to instantly derive the absolute lowest possible Sum of Squared Errors directly from the dataset.

By minimizing the squared outcome residuals at the observed input combinations, it finds the least-squares fit under the model.

Why Square the Errors?

. It stops positive (+4) and negative (-4) misses from canceling out to zero.

. It heavily punishes huge misses (a miss of 10 costs 100 points), forcing the model to compromise evenly across all variables.

Quick check · Can two identical columns have uniquely determined separate coefficients?

No. Their combined effect can be identified, but their separate weights cannot.

This is the most critical concept in multiple regression. What does a Study Boost (β1) actually mean when we have an optimal fit?

It means: If a student studies one extra hour, their score increases by exactly that boost amount—assuming their sleep and practice papers stay exactly the same.

This logic applies to each predictor's coefficient: β1 is the estimated association with Study while Sleep and Practice are held fixed, β2 is the estimated association with Sleep while Study and Practice are held fixed, and β3 is the estimated association with Practice while Study and Sleep are held fixed. These are not automatically causal effects.

The model does not literally freeze variables in real life—rather, it does this mathematically to estimate each variable's conditional association holding other modeled predictors fixed.

Notice the highlighted slice on the plane. We've locked "Sleep" at 6 hours (and Practice at its average). As we move purely along the Study axis, the score rises in a perfect straight line determined solely by β1.

Now we transition away from the 3D plane. In multiple regression, the prediction is built from a base intercept plus separate contributions from each predictor.

Each predictor adds or subtracts its own specific amount. The final score is simply the sum of all these individual parts. Because of how the model fits the data, each of these contributions is added while the other predictors are treated as fixed.

Predicting a specific student

  • Study 4.0 hrs
  • Sleep 7.5 hrs
  • Practice 2 pps
  • • Base Score = Starting point
  • • Study Boost = β1 × Study hours
  • • Sleep Boost = β2 × Sleep hours
  • • Practice Boost = β3 × Practice papers

Final Score = Sum of all parts

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

If multiple variables just stack, why not run a simple regression for each variable separately? Because of Spurious Correlations.

Imagine we want to predict Shark Attacks. If we run a simple model using Ice Cream Sales, the correlation is huge! It looks like ice cream causes shark attacks.

But the true driver is a lurking variable: High Temperature. Hot days cause both ice cream sales and beach visits (which lead to shark attacks).

The Power of "Holding Constant"

Multiple regression estimates a variable's

conditional association holding other modeled predictors fixed

, which can reveal when a variable only looked important because it moved together with another predictor. If we put both Ice Cream and Temperature into the model, the model essentially compares days with the

same temperature

. Holding temperature constant, ice cream sales have almost zero conditional association with shark attacks in this example.

If a coefficient shrinks close to zero after the other predictors are included, that suggests it has little

conditional

association with the predicted outcome (y) after accounting for the others. This does

not

prove the variable has no real-world effect at all, only that its estimated conditional association is small within this specific model.

Note: A larger coefficient doesn't always mean a "more important" predictor, as they depend on the unit of measurement.

Quick check · Should every available variable be adjusted for in causal work?

No. Mediators, colliders, and post-outcome variables need careful treatment.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

How do we know if our team of variables is actually good? R² tells us what percentage of the variance we explained.

For least-squares models with an intercept fitted to the same training observations, adding predictors makes training R² stay the same or increase. Even "Student's Shoe Size" may increase it, without improving predictions on new data.

Adj R² = 1 - [ (1 - R²)(n - 1) / (n - p - 1) ]

Adjusted R² corrects for this by penalizing model complexity. In the formula, n is the number of observations and p is the number of predictors.

Every new predictor (p) uses up flexibility and increases the penalty. Adjusted R² can rise or fall, even for an irrelevant variable in a particular sample. It is a descriptive aid, not an automatic feature-retention rule: consider validation performance, the modeling goal, and subject knowledge.

No. It rewards unnecessary flexibility.

Additional reference · worked numbers below are separate examples from the live notebook.

When a data analyst fits a multiple linear regression, they don't look at a 3D plot—they look at a summary table to evaluate the results.

  • Estimate: The coefficient (β). How much the prediction changes for a one-unit increase in this predictor, holding all other predictors constant.
  • Std. Error: The uncertainty around the estimate. A smaller standard error means we are more confident in the coefficient.
  • t value: The estimate divided by its standard error. A larger absolute value (typically > 2) indicates strong statistical evidence that the true effect is not zero.

Note: Each coefficient should be read as "the change in predicted y for a one-unit increase in this predictor, holding the others constant." Because predictors can be measured in entirely different units, coefficients cannot be compared as pure "importance scores" unless the predictors are on comparable scales.

Diagnostics belong with the output.

Inspect residual-versus-fitted, Q–Q, scale-location, leverage and influence plots. Use VIFs and coefficient intervals to diagnose multicollinearity; document categorical reference levels and interactions. Report confidence intervals for mean responses and wider prediction intervals for new observations. These coefficients are adjusted associations, not causal effects without a causal design. Multiple linear regression is a powerful detective. It models additive effects, isolates individual drivers, and finds the mathematically optimal fit by minimizing squared errors.
